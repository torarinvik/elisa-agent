# Research diary, 14 November 2026

## The encoder taught on pairs, at twice the size and read without labels

Exploratory, the same practice frames as yesterday (seed 7, grades 1 to 5, 250 each, 30,984 things); the
generator's figures used for scoring only. The encoder, the cutting of things from a frame and the scoring now
live in a package of their own, `nca-seeing`; `nca-choices` takes blobs, the page and the boxes from it.

Two things were changed from yesterday. Things are drawn at 64 by 64, not 32. And whether two things are the
same in a latent is read with no labels: over the pairs taught on, the distances in one latent are split into a
low and a high heap (Otsu, on a logarithmic scale), and a pair on the high side is read as different. Yesterday's
0.88, 0.89 and 0.77 were at the best cut found with the labels, over up to four latents, so today's are a
stricter measure and not to be set against them directly.

| Steps | Shape | Size | Fill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2,120 | 0.81 | 0.62 | 0.74 |
| 13,003 | 0.70 | 0.65 | 0.87 |

The share of 95,246 pairs for which the best single latent's reading agrees with the true difference; a second
latent added nothing. A pair differs in each attribute for a little under half of them, so 0.55 is what always
saying "the same" gives. After 13,003 steps the latents follow the attributes about as yesterday (shape 0.85
and 0.81 in two latents, size 0.75 in one, fill 0.55 and 0.45 in two), at 0.12 seconds a step against 0.034.

So the larger picture did not sharpen the reading, and the reading without labels is poor: the two heaps are
not apart. Small figures drawn up from few pixels were not the cause, or not the only one. What is left untried
is a teaching that asks for two heaps directly, such as codes that are whole numbers, and not for a picture
drawn back well.

Cutting the things took some five hours, not minutes: the resizing was written as one product over three
arrays, which numpy evaluates as a loop. It is now two products, 3 ms a thing, with the same result.

## Whether what one task leaves behind makes the next cheaper: a first test that shows little

Exploratory, seeds 0 to 2. The question is whether a learner already taught one task is taught another in fewer
takeovers. The learner of the matrix puzzles (`whole`, grade 3) was given number sequences from the Neural
Workshop's generator at level 2, where each term adds the same number: the last two terms and a candidate, named
as a row of a matrix is, six candidates.

| | Wrong untaught, of 300 | Takeovers on sequences | Wrong after, of 300 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taught sequences only | 257, 249, 252 | 2, 2, 2 | 0, 0, 0 |
| Taught matrices first | 257, 249, 252 | 1, 1, 1 | 0, 0, 0 |

Nothing carries untaught: the learner of matrices casts no vote on a sequence, and guesses. Taught, it needs one
takeover where a new learner needs two, and it then errs on 4, 7 and 11 of 300 matrices where it erred on few
before. The test cannot show much, because a new learner is taught this task in two takeovers: what carries
between the tasks is the pool of relations they are both given, not anything learned.

## Rules that stand on rules: preregistration

A task where a new learner fails was needed. Sequences at level 4 are polynomials of the second degree and at
level 5 of the third: the rule of level 2, of the steps and of the steps of steps. The pool has no probe for
either (THEORY 55.4, new today). `lifted` (THEORY 55.7, new today) offers, for each step in a probe a learner
looks at, the analogies between that step moved to every place: one storey up.

Four terms are shown, a candidate is the fifth, six candidates within four of the right one. Arms, one learner
for each level, differing in what it may ask:

- `flat`: the pool over the row of five (35 probes).
- `grown`: the pool and, before each level, the lift of what the learner of the level before looks at.
- `skipped`: as `grown`, but taught level 2 and then level 5.
- `matrices`: the pool and the lift of what a learner of matrices of grade 3 looks at, then as `grown`.
- `deep`: the pool and two lifts of all of it, laid out beforehand (41 probes).

On seeds 0 and 1, while building: `flat` is admitted at level 2 only and is wrong on 235 to 251 of 300 at
levels 4 and 5; `grown`, `matrices` and `deep` are admitted at every level in two takeovers with none wrong;
`skipped` is not admitted at level 5. Lifting everything is small here, because the places run out: a row of
five has 10 analogies, 5 more one storey up and 1 more above that. On a grid of four by four the analogies are
360, lifting all of them adds 520 and then 384, and lifting one held probe adds 140.

Predictions for fresh seeds 30 to 35, levels 2, 4 and 5, at most 300 cases taught, 300 audited:

1. `flat`: admitted at level 2 for all six seeds, at levels 4 and 5 for none.
2. `grown`: admitted at all three levels for all six, median takeovers at most 5 at each, at most 3 wrong of 300.
3. `skipped`: not admitted at level 5 for any seed.
4. `matrices`: admitted at levels 4 and 5 for all six.
5. `deep`: admitted at all three levels for all six, median takeovers within 2 of `grown`: on a row this short,
   growing the pool by what is learned buys nothing over laying it out.
6. Outside the lift: `deep` is not admitted at level 3 (multiply, then add) nor at level 6 (alternating, growing
   steps) for any seed.

If 2 holds and 5 holds, the gain is in what can be asked, and the claim that learning below is what makes the
storey above reachable rests on 3 and 4 only, on a task where it is not needed.

dear, such as a grid of four by four (360 analogies, 520 and 384 more for two lifts of all, 140 for the lift of
one held probe), and a task there whose rule stands a storey up. And a rule that multiplies is outside it.

## Rules that stand on rules, among twenty places: preregistration

Whether lifting only what is held pays where lifting everything is dear. The workshop's own matrices of four by
four do not ask it: `whole` is admitted at grade 11 in one or two takeovers through analogies between rows
(seeds 0 and 1, none wrong of 1,000). So the sequences are set in a grid: the asked sequence is the last of four
rows, the three above are other whole sequences of the same level from the generator, sharing the rule and
nothing else. Twenty places; the pool over them holds 1,140 probes, one lift of all of it 2,585 and two 4,125.
`grown` holds 1,140, 1,500 and 1,640 at the three levels.

On seeds 0 to 2, while building: `flat` is not admitted at levels 4 and 5 (167 and 170 wrong of 200, seed 0, 13
and 6 seconds); `grown` and `deep` are both admitted at every level in 2 or 3 takeovers, none wrong, in 0.1 to
0.2 seconds a level. So the expectation I stated to the user, that growing by what is held starts to pay here,
already looks wrong, and the predictions say so.

Predictions for fresh seeds 36 to 41, four rows, levels 2, 4 and 5, at most 200 taught, 200 audited:

1. `flat`: admitted at levels 4 and 5 for no seed.
2. `grown`: admitted at all three levels for all six, median takeovers at most 5.
3. `deep`: admitted at all three levels for all six, median takeovers within 1 of `grown` at each level, and no
   level taking more than a second. Lifting only what is held then buys a pool 2.5 times smaller and nothing that

## Skills three storeys high: development result and preregistration

Correction first. I told the user that installing what is learned as a part of what is learned next was not
built and that nothing showed parts compounding. Both were wrong: learned reuse and library curricula were
measured on 4 and 5 November, and on 11 November a taught skill offered as an action took composed skills from 2
of 16 admitted to 14 of 16. What had not been tried is a third storey.

A tower (`nca-skills` at `4b94491`) is a controller of two or three states that chooses between two development
composed skills, of different part sizes, and fixed actions: a controller over two controllers over four skills.
Its smallest machine without parts has 101 to 14,614 states. Three arms, by demonstration, each tower through the
gate once, teaching stopped at 1,000 takeovers: `tower` offers the two composed skills as taught with their own
parts; `tower-base` offers the four skills at the bottom and no composed skill; `tower-alone` offers none and
stops past 150 states.

## A tower whose parts are shown the action: diagnosis, development result and preregistration

Diagnosis first. The reserved result blamed parts that err rarely. Measured on 400 episodes of each development
tower, a composed skill that audits 127 or 128 of 128 on its own episodes errs or refuses in 0.5 to 15 percent of
the tower's episodes; where both audit 128 of 128 and the tower was cheap, they err in none. Each skill has an
environment of its own, and the tower draws other histories than the part was admitted on. A learner that only
tolerated the errors would inherit them and fail its own gate, so that was not built.

Built instead (§52.13, core at `0fd1c09`, `nca-skills` arm `tower-amended`): the composed skills are kept with the
episodes they and their parts were shown. Where the tower delegated to one and the teacher took over, the learner
tries the action as that skill's own right action, and so on down, and keeps the reading with the fewest states
in the tower and all its parts, the tower's own on a tie. The skills as installed are not changed. The arm stops
at 300 takeovers, because it merges several machines at each; `tower` stops at 1,000.

Development towers, 16:

| Arm | Admitted | Demonstrated actions | Takeovers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tower` | 8 | 41,387 | 7,125 |
| `tower-amended` | 10 | 11,817 | 1,644 |

It admits four towers `tower` did not, in 5, 5, 32 and 67 takeovers where `tower` stopped at 86 to 1,000, and
loses two that `tower` admitted: one passes its clean run after 91 takeovers and is refused at the gate, one
reaches 300 takeovers. Of the six both admit, four cost the same, one less (61 against 158) and one more (27
against 24). Of the four towers over a skill that was refused at its own gate it admits 2, `tower` 1. Taking the part's
reading on a tie as well was tried and was no better (9 admitted).

Predictions for a second reserved set, `further_towers`, draws 5 and 6 over the same composed skills, arms
`tower` and `tower-amended` each run once, nothing tuned afterwards:

1. `tower-amended` admits at least as many towers as `tower`, and 8 to 13 of 16.
2. `tower-amended` takes fewer than half the takeovers of `tower` in total.
3. Of the towers both admit, `tower-amended` takes no more takeovers than `tower` on at least three in four.
4. One to three towers admitted by `tower` are not admitted by `tower-amended`.
5. Every admitted tower audits at 122 of 128 or better.

## Parts taught where every history occurs: probe, development result and preregistration

Chosen after the third reserved set: the towers still not taught there all have a part that errs in the tower's
episodes, and showing parts the action treats that after the fact. Theory first (§51.6, §52.14, core at
`f9133eb`): a machine that errs at all errs on a short history, and in practice, where each observation is drawn
evenly whatever the action, no history has chance zero. So parts are taught in practice and not in a world of
their own; nothing else changes, and the tower is taught in its own world by the plain learner.

Probe, two development towers, seconds: the composed skills taught in practice erred in none of 300 of the
tower's episodes where those taught in their own worlds erred in 2 and 5 percent; one tower went from the limit
of 300 takeovers and 46 states to 20 takeovers and 3 states. All 16 towers were then probed in under two
minutes, both ways of teaching the parts together, and all were taught.

Development towers, 16, through the package (`nca-skills` at the commit that adds `tower-practised`):

| `tower-amended-on` | 11 | 14,080 | 1,956 | 178 | 130 |

Every tower of `tower-practised` audits 128 of 128 and has the 2 or 3 states of its rule. Its seconds include

Teaching the 16 composed skills with their parts took 1,449 takeovers in practice against 2,007 in their own

This is the benchmark's best case for the idea: its skills depend on the observations alone, so a teacher can be
asked for the action after any sequence of them. Where observations cannot be drawn freely the theory asks only
