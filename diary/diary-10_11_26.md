# Research diary, 10 November 2026

## State-machine learner: preregistration

The reasoning kinds that fail are mostly accumulations over a sequence, and enumeration cannot reach their routes (sizes 12 to 60 against a reach of 6 to 7). Test inference in its place, in a throwaway script: learn a machine whose state after reading a sequence fixes the answer.

Active arm: an observation-table learner (Angluin 1987, with Rivest and Schapire's counterexample step) asks the task for answers on sequences it chooses. Its alphabet is the symbols in the 32 training inputs. Counterexamples come from the training cases and from 100 random words per round. Limits per task: 8,000 queries, 150 states, 3 seconds. Every query is a label and is counted. Passive arm: greedy state merging on the 32 training cases only (RPNI, Oncina and García 1992), no queries. Both go to the unchanged gate, 135 cases at tolerance 5%, and a 128-case audit. Tasks with sequence outputs (rotate, cipher, dedupe) are skipped and counted as unserved, since each distinct output would need its own state. Answers come from each task's generator, standing in for a queryable task; inputs outside a task's domain return an undefined mark.

Predictions. Active: 12 to 22 of 60 served, among them all 5 boolean tasks and at least 3 counting tasks; every served task at least 122 of 128 on audit; hundreds to thousands of queries per served task. Passive: 0 to 3 served, because 32 random sequences share few prefixes. Baselines: tables 1 of 60, portfolio 1 of 60. Continue to an integrated learner only if the active arm serves at least 10 with clean audits.

### State-machine learner: result

The threshold is met at its minimum, and two predictions failed. Not integrated yet; one seed, development tasks, throwaway script.

| Arm | Served of 60 | Audit on served | Labels beyond the 32 training cases |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Active, chosen inputs | 10 | 128/128 on all 10 | 61 to 1,646 queries per served task; 99,766 over all 45 attempted |
| Passive, training cases only | 2 | 128/128 on both | none |
| Tables, earlier | 1 | 128/128 | none |
| Portfolio, earlier | 1 | 128/128 | none |

The active arm serves all 5 boolean tasks (2 to 4 states, 61 to 81 queries), 3 of 5 balance tasks (4 to 5 states, 84 to 144 queries) and 2 of 5 analogies (58 states, about 1,645 queries). I predicted 12 to 22 served and at least 3 counting tasks; it served 10 and no counting task. The gate rejected the other 3 learned analogy machines after 1, 15 and 31 cases. Learning took 0.14 seconds in total, so compute is negligible and labels are the whole cost. The balance machines have fewer states than exact tracking of depth needs, so they are probably approximations inside the tolerance, which is what the gate certifies. The passive arm serves boolean tasks 0 and 3 only, inside its predicted 0 to 3.

Why counting failed: 32 tasks stopped at the 150-state limit after as few as 301 queries, so the query limit was not what bound them. A count grows with the sequence, so over all sequences every new count is a new state and the table never closes. Finite-state machines are the wrong class for unbounded counts, as the legacy audit warned for its own inducer. The 15 sequence-output tasks were skipped as preregistered. Next candidate class, not yet tested: a finite control with a counter the transitions add to, which by their definitions would cover the counting tasks, the two axis modes of walk and the run-counting mode.

## Integrated state-machine learner: preregistration

The learner moves into the core as a portfolio learner for contracts with one sequence input, with the same limits as the test (8,000 queries, 150 states, 100 random checks per round). The harness gives it a task's answers on chosen inputs where the task has them; procedural families, SCAN and COGS have none and must be unchanged. One addition, untested so far: the first closure stops once it holds more states than the training labels have distinct values plus one, because by Theorem 51.2 more distinct answers than that already exceed the evidence. Sequence-output tasks are no longer skipped, so the rule has to stop them too.

Predictions on the 60 reasoning tasks. Table mode at 200,000 with the machine as a further ranked candidate: the same 10 served as in the test, all at least 122 of 128 on audit, and queries over all tasks below 40,000 against 99,766. Portfolio mode, where a fifth of the training cases is held out for selection: 9 to 11 served. Keep the learner if table mode serves at least 10 with clean audits; keep the stopping rule only if it loses no served task. D-50 in table mode stays at 19 served.

### Integrated state-machine learner: result

Kept, with one prediction failed. One seed, development tasks.

| 60 reasoning tasks | Served | Audit on served | Search work | Queries over all tasks | Gate labels |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tables, before | 1 | 128/128 | 203,149 | 0 | 230 |
| Tables with machines | 10 | 128/128 on all 10 | 203,149 | 52,072 | 1,602 |
| Portfolio, before | 1 | 128/128 | 6,053,100 | 0 | 135 |
| Portfolio with machines | 10 | 128/128 on all 10 | 5,953,100 | 55,607 | 1,424 |

Both modes serve the same 10 tasks as the throwaway test: 5 boolean rules, 3 balance tasks and 2 analogies. The portfolio result is inside its predicted 9 to 11, and it chose the machine on 9 tasks and a route on 1. D-50 in table mode stays at 19 served, SCAN 1 of 1 and COGS 0 of 1, and all 13 suites pass.

The query prediction failed: 52,072 against a predicted fewer than 40,000. The stopping rule lost no served task and cut counting from thousands of queries per task to 586 over all five, but it does not catch targets with few distinct answers and many states. Majority alone used 31,768 queries and analogies 12,230, and neither rule nor limit distinguishes them from learnable targets early. The 10 served tasks used 3,995 queries, 61 to 1,646 each. In table mode a served task now draws 135 to 180 gate cases, since a table candidate and a machine split the confidence; the three wrong analogy machines were rejected after 1, 16 and 16 cases.
