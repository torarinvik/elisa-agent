# Research diary, 10 November 2026

## State-machine learner: preregistration

The reasoning kinds that fail are mostly accumulations over a sequence, and enumeration cannot reach their routes (sizes 12 to 60 against a reach of 6 to 7). Test inference in its place, in a throwaway script: learn a machine whose state after reading a sequence fixes the answer.

Active arm: an observation-table learner (Angluin 1987, with Rivest and Schapire's counterexample step) asks the task for answers on sequences it chooses. Its alphabet is the symbols in the 32 training inputs. Counterexamples come from the training cases and from 100 random words per round. Limits per task: 8,000 queries, 150 states, 3 seconds. Every query is a label and is counted. Passive arm: greedy state merging on the 32 training cases only (RPNI, Oncina and García 1992), no queries. Both go to the unchanged gate, 135 cases at tolerance 5%, and a 128-case audit. Tasks with sequence outputs (rotate, cipher, dedupe) are skipped and counted as unserved, since each distinct output would need its own state. Answers come from each task's generator, standing in for a queryable task; inputs outside a task's domain return an undefined mark.

Predictions. Active: 12 to 22 of 60 served, among them all 5 boolean tasks and at least 3 counting tasks; every served task at least 122 of 128 on audit; hundreds to thousands of queries per served task. Passive: 0 to 3 served, because 32 random sequences share few prefixes. Baselines: tables 1 of 60, portfolio 1 of 60. Continue to an integrated learner only if the active arm serves at least 10 with clean audits.

### State-machine learner: result

The threshold is met at its minimum, and two predictions failed. Not integrated yet; one seed, development tasks, throwaway script.

| Arm | Served of 60 | Audit on served | Labels beyond the 32 training cases |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Active, chosen inputs | 10 | 128/128 on all 10 | 61 to 1,646 queries per served task; 99,766 over all 45 attempted |
| Passive, training cases only | 2 | 128/128 on both | none |
| Tables, earlier | 1 | 128/128 | none |
| Portfolio, earlier | 1 | 128/128 | none |

The active arm serves all 5 boolean tasks (2 to 4 states, 61 to 81 queries), 3 of 5 balance tasks (4 to 5 states, 84 to 144 queries) and 2 of 5 analogies (58 states, about 1,645 queries). I predicted 12 to 22 served and at least 3 counting tasks; it served 10 and no counting task. The gate rejected the other 3 learned analogy machines after 1, 15 and 31 cases. Learning took 0.14 seconds in total, so compute is negligible and labels are the whole cost. The balance machines have fewer states than exact tracking of depth needs, so they are probably approximations inside the tolerance, which is what the gate certifies. The passive arm serves boolean tasks 0 and 3 only, inside its predicted 0 to 3.

Why counting failed: 32 tasks stopped at the 150-state limit after as few as 301 queries, so the query limit was not what bound them. A count grows with the sequence, so over all sequences every new count is a new state and the table never closes. Finite-state machines are the wrong class for unbounded counts, as the legacy audit warned for its own inducer. The 15 sequence-output tasks were skipped as preregistered. Next candidate class, not yet tested: a finite control with a counter the transitions add to, which by their definitions would cover the counting tasks, the two axis modes of walk and the run-counting mode.

## Integrated state-machine learner: preregistration

The learner moves into the core as a portfolio learner for contracts with one sequence input, with the same limits as the test (8,000 queries, 150 states, 100 random checks per round). The harness gives it a task's answers on chosen inputs where the task has them; procedural families, SCAN and COGS have none and must be unchanged. One addition, untested so far: the first closure stops once it holds more states than the training labels have distinct values plus one, because by Theorem 51.2 more distinct answers than that already exceed the evidence. Sequence-output tasks are no longer skipped, so the rule has to stop them too.

Predictions on the 60 reasoning tasks. Table mode at 200,000 with the machine as a further ranked candidate: the same 10 served as in the test, all at least 122 of 128 on audit, and queries over all tasks below 40,000 against 99,766. Portfolio mode, where a fifth of the training cases is held out for selection: 9 to 11 served. Keep the learner if table mode serves at least 10 with clean audits; keep the stopping rule only if it loses no served task. D-50 in table mode stays at 19 served.

## Cutting wasted queries: diagnosis and preregistration

Of 52,072 queries in table mode, the 10 served tasks used 3,995. Majority used 31,768 and the rejected analogy machines 8,939. Two candidate savings were measured on the recorded run before choosing.

A coverage check from training alone, the Good–Turing estimate of the chance that an input holds a symbol never seen, is not adopted. It flags four next-term tasks clearly (0.25 to 0.74 against true rates 0.23 to 0.68) and would save about 1,640 queries, 3%. Near the tolerance it is unreliable: it gives 0.061 for a served analogy whose true rate is 0, and 0 for two rejected analogies whose true rates are 0.12 and 0.06, because symbols in fixed positions are not independent draws.

A uniform cap is the lever (49.2), and nothing found distinguishes majority from a learnable target early. Replaying the recorded costs under a per-task cap:

| Cap per task | Served | Queries over all tasks |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 128 | 7 | 4,766 |
| 256 | 8 | 7,396 |
| 1,024 | 8 | 18,266 |
| 2,048 | 10 | 26,533 |
| 8,000 | 10 | 52,072 |

Implement the round robin of 49.2 and 49.3 as a generic core function: one total query budget, caps doubling from 1, resumption free because answers are kept, and an attempt that stops below its cap is dropped. Predict in table mode: a budget of 27,000 serves the same 10 with clean audits; a budget of 7,500 serves 8, the boolean and balance tasks. The default becomes 450 queries per queryable task, 27,000 here, chosen on these development tasks as the smallest round that keeps every served task. Portfolio mode runs tasks in separate processes and keeps its per-task limit. Keep if both predictions hold and D-50 stays at 19.

## Counter class: check and preregistration

Class: a finite control whose transitions add to one register, with the register as the answer. Then the one-step difference `f (w ++ [a]) - f w` depends only on the control state and the symbol, so the difference is a finite-state function of the sequence and the existing machine learner can learn it. The answer is rebuilt by summing differences along the sequence from the answer on the empty sequence.

Check on the generators before any learning, 300 sampled inputs per kind: one-step differences take 2 values for counting, runs, boolean rules and balance, 3 for walk, 7 for majority and over 30 for next term and order statistics; analogies and the sequence-output kinds have none. Reading the hidden modes, the class should hold for all 5 counting tasks, the 4 walk tasks that track one axis and the 3 run tasks that count runs, 12 tasks that are unserved today. It should not hold for distance, longest run or peak depth, whose differences depend on unbounded state.

Test in a throwaway script: learn the difference with the core machine learner, at most 4,000 difference queries and 150 states, rebuild answers by summing, and send the result to the unchanged gate. A training case the rebuilt answer gets wrong adds its prefixes as cases, and learning repeats. Every answer asked of the task is a label. Predictions: at least 10 of those 12 tasks served, each at least 122 of 128 on audit and under 1,000 labels; with the 10 served already, at least 20 of 60. Continue to integration if at least 8 new tasks are served with clean audits.

## Integrated counter learner: preregistration

The difference learner moves into the core beside the finite-state learner. The two share one cache of a task's answers, so an answer either has asked is free for the other, and training labels are never asked again. In table mode a task's two learners split its allowance in each doubling round; one that stops below its limit passes its unused share to the other, and the first machine found is the task's candidate. The repair loop has no fixed count: it ends when a repair adds no new case or the budget ends. Limits stay 8,000 queries per learner and 150 states; the default total stays 450 per queryable task, 27,000 here, and is not retuned.

Predictions for the 60 reasoning tasks in table mode, with every served task at least 122 of 128 on audit: at 54,000 queries 23 to 25 served; at the default 27,000, 19 to 23, since tasks that never finish take half of each round; at 7,500, 10 to 18. Portfolio mode, per-task limits: 23 to 25. D-50 stays at 19, SCAN 1 of 1, COGS 0 of 1. Keep if at least 20 are served at 54,000 with clean audits.

## Allowance between two learners of one goal: preregistration

Measured first, each learner alone at 8,000 queries on the 60 development reasoning tasks. On a target outside its class
the finite learner usually stops early (12 to 155 queries on 30 of the 45 tasks it fails, about 600 on runs, several
thousand on majority). The difference learner declines for free on sequence answers and after about 80 queries on
analogies, and otherwise runs to its limit. Summed over the 25 servable tasks, finite then difference costs about 22,600
queries and difference then finite about 19,700; the equal split pays up to twice the cheaper success.

Rule to test: attempts at one goal run in turn. The first takes the whole allowance; the next starts only when the one
before stops below its limit. Two orders are tried, in table mode with the same three query budgets.

Predictions:

| Order | 7,500 | 27,000 | 54,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal split (now) | 3 | 21 | 25 |
| Finite, then difference | 8–10 | 20–23 | 25 |
| Difference, then finite | 3–5 | 22–25 | 25 |

Keep an order only if it serves at least 8 at 7,500, at least 21 at 27,000 and 25 at 54,000, with every served task at
122/128 or better on audit. If both pass, keep the one serving more in total. The order is a prior chosen on development
data; turn-taking loses the split's factor-two guard when the first learner fails slowly and the second would succeed
quickly, as on runs.
