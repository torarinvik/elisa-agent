# Research diary, 22 November 2026

## Two tasks in one quick reader

Carried on from last night's last entry: in one learner of the reflex
task (Quick Press) and the two-step maze, a quick reader taught from
nothing on all 20,184 lessons reads the reflex task at 0.614 with no
closer look, and taught on that task's 2,781 lessons alone at 0.913.
The tasks interfere in the one reader.

**Literature (08:47).** This is the crosstalk that Rueckl, Cave and
Kosslyn (1989) found when one network learned what a thing is and
where it is, and that Jacobs, Jordan and Barto (1991, Cognitive
Science 15:219) removed with several networks and a gate that compete
for each pattern: taught on the gate-weighted mixture of their
outputs with the likelihood as loss, each pattern's gradient goes
mostly to the network that already reads it best, the gate learns to
give it that network, and the task falls apart into networks of its
own with no one saying where to cut (Jacobs, Jordan, Nowlan and Hinton
1991, "Adaptive mixtures of local experts"). Multi-task reinforcement
learning finds the same interference in a shared network and treats
it either in the optimiser (projected gradients, PCGrad, Yu et al.
2020; CAGrad, Liu et al. 2021) or by modules and routing (soft
modularization, Yang et al. 2020; orthogonal experts, MOORE, Hendawy
et al. 2024), the latter reported to remove what the former only
lessens. Routing by the input alone, with no task named, is known to
drift as tasks accumulate (StaR-MoE and others, 2025 to 2026). The
picture's "concepts 1 … n" is this: several readers, each with its
hypotheses, not one.

A point of mathematics that makes it cheap here: for a lesson paid or
not, the cross-entropy of the mixed chance Σ gᵢ pᵢ is exactly the
mixture's loss −log Σ gᵢ ℓᵢ, since 1 − Σ gᵢ pᵢ = Σ gᵢ (1 − pᵢ) where the
gᵢ sum to one. So a reader that gives the logit of the mixed chance is
taught by the learner's teaching as it stands, and nothing else in the
learner changes.

So a batch is near what the device does at this size, and what costs
is how many batches a run teaches. Each teaching goes twice over all a
reader's lessons, and one comes at every growth by a tenth, so over a
run of `N` lessons a reader is taught on about `2 N (1 + g) / g` of them,
22 times each with `g` a tenth.

## Reckoning ahead
