### Idea 1: trust as the reading of each source under which the dear answers do not move the chances. Theory and the statement for a test of seconds (08:21)

Theory. An inquiry stands at what its sources have said. Let the chance of each answer be the pool of the sources' words, each to its weight: log-chance of answer k is Σ over sources of w(s)·o(s, k), up to the common term. Weight nought is trusting nobody: every answer equally likely whatever was said. Three facts carry the learning.

(a) A dear answer is a fair name for the standing it was asked at. The rule asks from the standing alone (place, chances, record), so given the standing, whether the dear source is asked is independent of which answer is right: the share right among the standings asked at is the share right among all standings like them. A try (§76.14) is the same case.

(b) Counting agreement where one asked is not fair for a source. Two sources each right with chance p, the dear source asked where they disagree: among those trials each is right exactly half the time, whatever p. An asker that counts so comes to trust nobody for ever. The earlier standings of a trial the rule later chose to name are selected on what followed and are not fair draws either.

(c) A trial named by a coin tossed apart from everything is a fair draw at every standing it passed (§38.1's coin, the label now an asking at its charge; by 76.14 it gives up at most the charge).

So the weights are fitted by likelihood on the standing asked at for every dear answer the rule or a try brought, and on every standing passed for trials the coin named. At the fitted weights the dear answers do not move the chances in the mean, source by source: that is the score equation, and it is §76.16's condition asked of the one asking that ends all drift. A weak pull to the starting weight keeps few names from carrying far.

Test of seconds, before any core code: the weighing asker of the night goal with no practice loads. Weights begin at nought; the person's word is believed at the 0.99 its expert states; the record begins empty, 10 tries a cell; no coin. Scratch code, the frame and `Learning` unchanged. ### Idea 3 of item 2: an asking that may be made only so often. Statement (08:40)

Theory. Let a way of asking be paid P and make the limited asking R times an inquiry. ### Theory, and two tests of seconds (09:36)

§76.20, a judgement of the answer taken. What comes back in acting is whether the answer given was paid. Fair for the place and band it was given at, as a dear answer is (76.17 i); and it is the last-place value of 76.13 itself, what answering there is had, read from pay and not from the stated chance. So the record keeps, for a place and band, how many answers given there were judged and what they were paid, and values answering there by that share, pulled toward the chance with the weight of eight answers (hand-chosen). What it cannot teach: which of the answers not taken was right; an answer never taken somewhere is never corrected there, and trying one costs what a wrong answer costs, not a charge.

§76.21, an asking that takes time. If an asking is followed with chance h by the inquiry being over with nothing had, it is worth (1 − h) of what is had where the inquiry goes on: the share of askings that came too late is a charge of the form of 76.11, counted and not stated, and it differs from place to place and from task to task as the counts do. Thought pays exactly where the share of what it adds exceeds the share of times it comes too late. The record holds this as it stands once an inquiry that ended during an asking is recorded as led to a last place where nothing is had.

### 12:27 After the report: the lever item 1 lacks, worked through before building. A trial by turns

The user: go on to the lever. I named it as "read a dearer asking's gain from the step with a taught reading".
Worked through, that is not what the numbers ask for. What bounded item 1 was information: about a hundred looks
in 6,000 presses, against the two hundred a gain of a tenth needs at two standard errors. A reading taught on
those same hundred looks does not make more of them. What sets their number is the pace of tries, and 76.23 (iii)
left the pace open ("any pace that grows without bound and slower than the visits serves").

Theory. Let a dearer asking gain d over the plain choice at a standing, with scatter s of what trials come to.
Tried to the square root of N visits it has √N makings, and is told apart at z standard errors once
√N ≥ (z s / d)², that is after N ≥ (z s / d)⁴ visits: for d = 0.1, s = 0.45, z = 2, some 6,500 visits. Tried by
turns with the plain choice (every other visit, until decided) it has N / 2 makings and is told apart after
N ≈ 2 (z s / d)² visits: some 160. The trial ends in one of two ways: the asking is told apart as better, and is
taken from then on; or its gain is told to be under a smallest gain worth looking for, δ, and it is left. Where
the true gain is nought that takes about 2 (z s / δ)² visits, after which nothing more is spent; the square
root's tries never end. What the trial costs while it runs: half the visits times what the asking loses there,
for a number of visits that does not grow. And it is a fair comparison, which tries made at the rule's
convenience are not: the two arms meet the same standings.
Hand-chosen: δ = 0.05, a twentieth of a trial (the same size as the thinker's loss of a step, which this is not:
it is taken off nothing, it only says what gain is too small to go on testing for). Only a decision's first
making of an asking is tried by turns; a repeat stays at the square root.
A taught reading of the gain stays the next refinement, for choosing among the steps of one band; it is not
built here.

paid, four steps a trial, no closer look (it gains nothing there, and about 650 decisions should tell that it

### 12:34 Small case, turns by step: FAILED its prediction, and why. Turns by decision

1. The turns were by step. A decision that took the plain choice at its first step stood at the same place at
   its second, where it was the look's turn: every trial had a look, and "plain" was "look one step later".
   The two arms must differ in the whole decision, not in one step of it.
2. A line of two standard errors read at every making is crossed by chance about once in five over some hundreds
   of makings (for a walk read from 10 to 600 steps, about (c - 1/c) φ(c) ln(T) / 2 = 0.17 at c = 2 and 0.025 at
   c = 3), and once crossed the plain choice was never counted again, so the mistake stayed.

to an asking makes it, by one more coin, at a place it comes to where the count has not decided; a decision
given to the plain choice makes no asking on trial. Only those decisions, and the steps after an asking was
made, are counted as plain. A first making is taken at three standard errors (hand-chosen, FIRM), left where
its gain is under a twentieth at two; where it is taken, the plain choice is still counted on to the square
root of the decisions there (76.23 iii, for the plainer option). A closer look made before any reader of looks
is taught is not counted (the core does not record an asking the kernel gave nothing for).
What it costs to tell: for a gain d and scatter s, FIRM² s² (1/n₁ + 1/n₀) ≤ d², with a quarter of decisions
making the look at a given place and a half plain: n₁ ≈ 13.5 s²/d² ... for d = 0.2, s = 0.45 about 70 looks,

### 13:36 The user: build the gain read from the step. Theory and the small case, written first

What the count by place cannot do. A place (reader, band of the best chance, askings made) holds every step
that falls in it. Let the steps of a place be of kinds i with shares w_i and gains d_i of a dearer asking. The
count learns the mean, Σ w_i d_i, and takes the asking at all of them or none: it is paid max(Σ w_i d_i, 0)
over the plain choice where taking it at the kinds that gain is paid Σ w_i max(d_i, 0). For two kinds, +a at a
share w and -b at the rest, the count has max(w a - (1 - w) b, 0) of the w a there is; with a = b and w = ½ it
has nothing. Seed 1 of the schedule is that: one reader for a task where the look gains and one where it costs.

What is built. For each reader and each dearer asking, two taught readings in the form going on is read
(what the reader made of the step, the best chance, how long since a judgement): the chance the trial ends
paid after a first making of the asking here, taught on the steps where it was made, and after the plain choice
here, taught on the steps counted as plain before any dearer asking. Their difference is the gain as read. It
decides nothing. It sorts: a step's place for that asking is (reader, loses | about nought | gains, askings
made), by the read gain against ∓SMALL, in place of the band of the best chance, once both readings have had
FIRST lessons. Each sorted place has its own count and its own trial by turns, read alone (the bands of the
best chance were weighed together; places the reading says differ are not). So 76.23 and 76.24 hold in each
place as before, whatever the reading is worth: a step is sorted before its trial's outcome is known, by
readings taught on earlier trials. A reading that tells nothing splits one count in three and costs up to three
times the makings; one that tells the kinds apart gives each its own verdict.
Hand-chosen: three sorts, cut at ∓SMALL (no new number); FIRST = 64 lessons of each before sorting.

**Small case, written first.** A toy of two ports and two kinds of trial under one reader, each decided at its

Why a place-by-place reading cannot have it. Let a frame be a set of things, each a picture and a place. The reader's pairing reads, at each place, the frame of now, an earlier frame and their difference there. ### Paper E, analysis: a first examination passed, set by myself (21:38)

Built as `nca_phonetics.analysis` (package 0.15.0, operation `phonetics/analysed`): from a language's words in transcription, which sounds contrast (a pair of words for each) and which two sounds are one phoneme said two ways, with the rule in the alphabet's terms. The idea, which is the analyst's own procedure made exact: join two sounds only when (1) they are near by what the alphabet says of them, (2) no two words differ in them alone and they never stand between the same neighbours, (3) one of them is confined to neighbours of a class the alphabet can name, the fewest terms said of all those neighbours and of none of the other's, (4) keeping apart so cleanly would happen by chance less than once in twenty for each class tried (with n meetings of the confined sound among N of both, one deal in N choose n is the clean one), and (5) the confined sound has a reason to be as it is there: the edge of a word, or what it has and the other lacks is said of its neighbours or follows from them.

The first draft, with (1) to (3) alone, found the rule in six of eight kinds of made language and joined sounds by accident on every small real list (`o` with `u`, `n` with `r`); (4) and (5) were added for that, and (1) became a graded distance.

### The analysis mended on the thirteen, and a third examination set (23:25)

Why it failed, problem by problem: it asked each pair of sounds for enough evidence on its own, and a list of fifteen words never has it; it took the nearest pair first whatever the evidence; it knew too few reasons; and the chart's `a` counted as a front vowel.

What an examinee does instead, and the analysis now does:
- Evidence is counted as odds. The ways the meetings of two sounds could have been dealt out stand against the classes tried and against how likely such a change is beforehand: a change with a reason in its surroundings is looked for in few places (4, times 8 for each further term of the class); one without a reason has each pair's ways divided by 100 and the classes tried counted in full. Joined at odds of 20.
- Pairs that change alike under one rule are one rule and their ways multiply (Kongo `t s z` before `i`: 10, 4 and 35 ways, none enough alone). A sound met once is joined only beside a judged pair that changes in exactly the same way.
- A sound is a way of saying the nearest phoneme it could be one of; one rule gives a phoneme one way in one place; a phoneme said three ways gets as its home the way met in the most surroundings from which the others can be told.
- Reasons added: a stop ends in friction before a close vowel; a fricative is made at the lips before a rounded vowel; a velar after a vowel that is not front; a liquid or nasal, or a sound that differs by a mark, has its own way before a vowel; what a sound gains for standing last (voiceless, unreleased, velarized) or first (aspirated) in a word. An obstruent does not take its neighbour's place and does not change manner, voicing or part of the mouth without a reason; nasals and liquids may.
- A plosive and an affricate are a half step apart; vowel height and backness count a half for each step of the chart; the chart's open front unrounded vowel is counted as neither front nor back.

The analysis joins sounds readily when the change has a reason in its surroundings, and the reasons are a table and a function I wrote by hand (`MOTIVES`, `natural`), mended after each examination; each mending carried to about a third of the next unseen set. An expert's sense of what is a likely change is itself learned from the languages they have met. So the test: build the reasons from the keys of solved problems and nothing else.

A reason is a triple: what the confined sound gains or loses against its phoneme's home way (one term, with its sign), the side (before, after, beside), and one term said of every neighbour on that side where the confined sound stands. From a solved problem, for each sound its key joins to a phoneme, every such triple whose neighbour term is said of none of the other ways' neighbours on that side is counted, once a problem. A rule is then held to have a reason when one of its triples was met in a solved problem. The rest of the analysis (odds, pooling, counterexamples, what may change with no reason) stays as it is, and it was tuned on these same problems; only the reasons are held out, which I say so as not to claim more.
