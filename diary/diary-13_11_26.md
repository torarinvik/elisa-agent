# Research diary, 13 November 2026

## Joins and likenesses under one test: result

The preregistration is the last entry of 12 November. Snake pixels, fresh seeds 128 to 139. Both learners admitted
on all 12. As shipped, strict joins and no likeness: a median of 95.5 takeovers (24 to 217), 1190 in all. Joins and
likenesses under the one test: a median of 95 (33 to 136), 1012 in all, fewer on 8 seeds and more on 4; every seed
ended with the four directions in one group holding one mirror. Admission, the median without, the count of seeds
and the structure held; the median with likeness was predicted at 50 to 90. By the rule set beforehand, fewer on at
least 9 seeds, likeness stays off in Snake.

The one test does what it was made for: no action is left apart with likenesses of its own, where five of twelve
seeds ended so before. It does not make a mirror pay on Snake. The medians are equal, and the gain is in the
longest teachings only. With the four directions joined, a mirror can at most halve the classes of a view of two
to six probes; against that stand a pivot to be found and a view learned afresh whenever a join or likeness
changes. On Othello, with one action and all eight turns to gain from, the same mechanism took a quarter off the
takeovers.

## Looking for a block whose side is known (THEORY §54.11)

Under a budget of 512 pixels a frame few pixel agents passed the gate. Exploratory, tuning seeds 0 to 5, agents taught
on the whole screen and playing 3,000 moves alone under the budget: the food was unfound in 4.2% to 5.3% of frames,
and the person objected to 50 to 81 of the moves made there, about half, against 17 to 34 of the 2,850 made with the
food found. Each time the food was eaten, 120 looks went to rings around where it had been and the rest to a random
scatter, which finds nine pixels among 4,096 in about 410 looks on average.

`Gaze` now measures the side of each block when it first finds it. Its rings step by that side, 15 looks for 120
here, and a missing block is looked for at the positions whose coordinates are multiples of its side, 484 here,
before the scatter. The same agents and moves: the food unfound in 0 to 6 frames of 3,000, 18 to 30 objections in
all, 251 to 274 eaten where it was 234 to 259, and 84 to 91 pixels read a frame where it was 122 to 127. Agents taught
under the budget, tuning seeds 0 to 7: 7 of 8 admitted with 35 to 93 takeovers, the eighth not after 252. At a budget
of 128 the food stays unfound in 12% to 17% of frames: 484 positions are several frames of looks.

## Whether what is read from blocks carries to another board size (THEORY §54.6)

The probes of 54.6 read a pixel at an offset from a block's corner and which way one block lies from another.
Neither reads a fixed place, so a rule over them should not depend on how large the screen is. That has not been
measured: every teaching and every gate so far was on a board of 16 cells a side.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 348 to 371, Snake from pixels on the whole screen, turns on,
steady off, the scripted person, a limit of 5,000 moves, taught on a board of 16 cells a side. Each learner is then
left untaught and gated on fresh games on boards of 16, 32 and 8 cells a side, once by the person who taught it and
once by a person who lets dead ends pass, and plays 2,000 moves alone on each board, the teacher's objections
counted by cause: a crash, a dead end, or food left where another move would not leave it. The claim holds if the
learners passing the teacher's gate at 32 are at most two fewer than those passing it afresh at 16, and those
passing the lenient gate at 8 are at most two fewer than those passing it at 16. Prediction: 21 to 24 pass the
teacher's fresh gate at 16 and as many, within two, at 32, with no more objections in 2,000 moves at 32 than at 16;
at 8 fewer than 16 pass the teacher's gate, dead ends being the most common objection there, since a snake on 64
cells closes itself in far sooner and the probes do not show a dead end; 20 to 24 pass the lenient gate at 8.

Result. All 24 were admitted in teaching, with a median of 49.5 takeovers.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 17 | 22 | 18.5 | 21.8 | 8.6 | 3.8 | 9.4 | 170.5 |

The first part of the claim held: 24 pass at 32 where 17 pass afresh at 16. The second failed: 4 pass the lenient

at 32 where as many within two; no more objections at 32 held; fewer than 16 at 8 held, with one; dead ends were

at 8 where 20 to 24 were predicted. A learner admitted once fails a fresh gate on the same board about three times
in ten: the admission is close to its bar.

What is read from blocks carries to a larger board and not to a smaller one. Exploratory, seeds 348 to 355, the same
learners playing 4,000 moves alone at 16 and at 8. Their views hold 2 to 5 probes. Of 1,304 objections at 8, 72 were
at readings not met in the 4,000 moves at 16. Had each reading met on both boards drawn objections at 8 at its rate
at 16, 889 would be expected where 1,232 occurred. So about two thirds of the failures on the small board are
readings that already fail as often on the large one and come up more, a quarter are the same readings failing more
often, and a twentieth are readings not met before. At most 10 moves in 4,000 were on a board seen before; the
snake does not circle. On seed 348 each of the 22 crashes at 8 came where no safe move was judged good: every move
then has a bad majority and one is guessed, the fatal ones among them.

Exploratory, not kept: on the same learners, taking the move with the largest share of good votes where none is
judged good. In 2,000 moves at 8, crashes 243 to 77, dead ends 125 to 194, food left 301 to 308, 669 to 579 in
all; at 16, crashes 74 to 28, dead ends 31 to 61, food left 104 to 120, 209 to 209 in all. The crashes avoided
become dead ends entered a few moves on.

The readings do not depend on the board (THEORY 54.12); what the learner meets does. On a small board a long snake
is beside itself and the wall most of the time, and there the teacher's verdict turns on what a view of a few
pixels beside the head does not hold: whether a move leaves room, and which of several moves judged bad is least
so. This is a limit of deciding from the present frame, not of reading by blocks.

## Relations between what is read (THEORY §55)

A learner may now be given probes that read whether two slots hold the same value and which holds the greater
(`learning/relations.py`). Two tasks not written for this were taken to measure them. The first is the `query`
shape of the record-resolution package: among the records of a log, the latest of the wanted entity at or before
a cutoff; entity identifiers of 32 bits and times of 64 are drawn afresh in every case, so no value recurs. The
second is the first grade of the Neural Workshop's matrix puzzles, a grid of two by two panels that all hold one
picture, the last to be picked from four; a panel is given as what a reader of its figures would report, the
shape, size, fill, number, angle and places, and panels are named by where they stand from the missing one. In
both the learner chooses among candidates, is taken over when wrong, and is gated on 300 cases it was not taught,
new ones at each gate, after every 50 cases taught.

Exploratory, tuning seeds 1000 to 1003. Records: with the relations the learner was admitted after 2 to 6
takeovers and made no error in 1,000 further cases; with values only it was taken over about 1,800 times in 2,000
cases. Matrices, seed 1000: admitted after 10 takeovers, no error in 1,000, and none in 1,000 puzzles in the colour
palette, untaught; with values only, 1,416 takeovers and 757 wrong in 1,000. Two things were found on the way. A
preference must be recorded before the proposal is judged bad: slots that are the same for every candidate of a
case otherwise make each of them look bad, no rival is left, and no direction is learned. And a set over values
that never recur was the measure by which others were dropped (THEORY 53.5); that is corrected in `Relevance`,
with a test, though these runs come out the same without it.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 0 to 23, a limit of 2,000 cases, 1,000 further cases audited.
Records: `Relevance`, sets of at most two, a share kept of 0.3, the time ordered; the relating learner has the six
values and three relations as components, the valuing learner the six values. The relations are kept if the
relating learner is admitted on at least 22 seeds with a median of at most 15 takeovers and at most 10 wrong in
1,000 on each admitted seed, and the valuing learner is admitted on none. Matrices, grey palette: `Inquiry`, sets
of at most three, a share kept of 0.3, a shortlist of 24; the relating learner has 54 values, 216 sameness and 108
order probes, the valuing learner the 54 values. Kept if the relating learner is admitted on at least 22 seeds
with a median of at most 25 takeovers, at least nine in ten of those admitted pass a gate on puzzles in the colour

## The same step (THEORY §55.5)

second slot the same way and as far from the first, take the same step: 52 probes for nine places in a square. For

## Looking ahead (THEORY §56)

The learners of the board-size entry fail on a small board where the present frame does not decide. `foresight`
carries a choice forward through what follows an action: an action lasts `d` steps if it is not ruled out and some
action lasts `d − 1` steps after it, and the choice is made among the actions that last longest.

Exploratory, seeds 348 to 355, learners taught on 16 cells a side playing 1,000 moves alone on 8, the game's own
rule saying what follows a move, with food after a meal placed by another draw than the game's. Ruling out what
the learner judges bad changed nothing: 338 objections in all without looking ahead, 338 at two steps, 359 at
four. Where it matters every move has a bad majority, because a bad judgement mostly means the teacher preferred
another move, not that the move fails; no move then lasts at all. Ruling out only what ends the game: crashes 111,
3, 5 and 2 at depths 0, 2, 4 and 8; games ended 143, 69, 47 and 31; but dead ends objected to 60, 116, 109 and
101, food left 167, 218, 256 and 264, objections in all 338, 337, 370 and 367, and food eaten 1,237, 1,121, 966
and 894. The snake lives longer, is longer, and is then more often where the teacher objects.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 372 to 383, taught on 16, 1,000 moves alone on 8, ruling out
what ends the game, depth 4 against none. Looking ahead is kept in the core, as a way of avoiding ends that can be
foreseen, if the games ended at depth 4 are at most half those without it in all and fewer on at least 10 of the
12 learners. Prediction: games ended 25 to 45% of those without; objections in all 95 to 125%; food eaten 70 to
88%. It is not expected to bring a learner through the teacher's gate.

## What teaching alone can rule out (THEORY §56.3)

Looking ahead needs pairs that truly fail. The last entry took them from the end of the game. A learner's own
judgements hold some as well: a move whose readings were judged bad and never once good is not a matter of
preference. Exploratory, seeds 348 to 355, four steps ahead on 8 cells a side, no end shown to the search, what
follows a move that ends the game treated as unknown: crashes objected to 111 without looking ahead and 58 with,
games ended 143 and 92, objections in all 338 and 348. Allowing a share of good votes up to 2% did worse, 79
crashes and 106 games. The end of the game as the mark gave 5 crashes and 47 games on these learners.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 384 to 395, taught on 16, 1,000 moves alone on 8, four steps
ahead ruling out the moves never judged good, against none. The rule is worth stating if games ended are at most
80% of those without in all and fewer on at least 9 of the 12 learners. Prediction: games ended 55 to 75%,
objections in all 95 to 115%.

Result, fresh seeds 384 to 395: games ended 192 without looking ahead and 148 with, 77%, fewer on 11 of the 12
learners; crashes objected to 139 and 98; objections in all 423 and 386; food eaten 1,935 and 1,817. The bar was
met, narrowly. Both predictions missed: games ended were 77% where 55 to 75% was predicted, and objections 91%
where 95 to 115%. What a learner was taught never to do rules out about a quarter of the ends that the end of the
game itself rules out three fifths of: a fatal move whose readings the teaching never met is not ruled out, and a
view of a few pixels beside the head meets only some of them. Nothing here foresees the teacher's objection to a
dead end or to food left; those are not failures of the move, and the view does not hold what they turn on.

## A reading of how much room a place has (exploratory, not kept)

Tried outside the repository: a probe giving the number of positions reached from a place beside an anchor through
positions holding what that place holds, in steps as long as the offset, counted up to 12, offered for every value
and offset beside the pixel probes of 54.6 and turned with them. Taught on 16 cells a side, seeds 348 to 355, no
learner took one up, and seven of the eight came out as without them. Taught on 8 cells a side, where the teacher
objects to dead ends far more often, seeds 348 to 351 and a limit of 2,000 moves: again none taken up, and none
admitted with or without.

The teacher's verdict is not a function of the room alone. It is whether the room is at least the snake's length,
a comparison of two counts, and a count read as a symbol has a class for each number and each length. What would
express it is an order probe of 55 between two counts, the room and the number of positions a value holds. Neither
count is a reading the core offers yet.

## The fourth and fifth grades of the matrices (THEORY §55.5)

The exploratory runs of these grades were admitted after 6 to 10 takeovers, few for two and three rules among
eight candidates, and it was not known what the learners read. Preregistration, written before running. Fresh
seeds 0 to 23, the learner with the same step, a limit of 1,000 puzzles, 1,000 audited. The grades count as taught
if on each at least 22 are admitted, with at most 10 wrong in 1,000 on each admitted seed of the fourth and a
median of at most 20 on the fifth. As a check that the matrix is read and not the candidates alone, each admitted
learner also chooses in 1,000 puzzles whose shown panels are those of the next puzzle while the candidates are its
own; the check holds if every learner is then wrong in at least 600, where a guess among eight is wrong in 875.
Prediction: all 24 admitted on both grades after a median of 7 to 11 takeovers; wrong in 1,000 a median of 0 to 3
on the fourth grade and 5 to 20 on the fifth; with the panels exchanged, 750 to 900 wrong.

Result, fresh seeds 0 to 23.

| Grade | Admitted | Takeovers, median | Fewest and most | Wrong in 1,000, median | Most | Wrong with panels exchanged, median | Fewest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 24 | 9 | 6, 14 | 0 | 19 | 872.5 | 851 |
| 5 | 24 | 10 | 7, 17 | 7.5 | 22 | 876.5 | 854 |

The fourth grade missed its bar: one of the 24 admitted learners was wrong in 19 of 1,000 where at most 10 was
required of each; the other 23 made no error. The fifth met its bar, and the check held on both: with the shown panels exchanged for another puzzle's
every learner fell to a guess, so the matrix is what is read. The views hold a median of seven probes. Of the
probes in all views on the fourth grade, 73 ask whether the candidate is the same as a panel in an attribute, 88
whether two pairs take the same step, and 3 anything else; on the fifth 57, 113 and 3. Two values of the candidate
itself are read on the fourth grade and one on the fifth. Every prediction held.

So a few takeovers do teach these grades, by the relations and not by the candidates, though not always to the standard
of the lower grades: on the fifth grade a learner admitted within its allowance is left with up to two errors in
a hundred, and one on the fourth.
