## Probe: slots as packaged and reworked, taught from the start on hands_off (development)

The scratch copy of yesterday's last entry (among chosen steps the one given the least
chance of being of the kind is let go, a step given less than every one kept is not kept,
steps chosen before the last judgement go first) beside the package's reader (`e401315`),
three slots, reach 12, seeds 6301 to 6316: four plays of 150 seconds at rung 1, four at
rung 2, eight at rung 3, 900 untaught steps after each rung, side by side with one other
process. Share of judgements paid, chance 0.25:

| | package | reworked |
|---|---|---|
| rung 1, untaught | 37 of 73 (0.507) | 37 of 73 (0.507) |
| rung 2, untaught | 9 of 48 (0.188) | 13 of 34 (0.382) |
| rung 3, while taught, eight plays | 89 of 1685 (0.053) | 165 of 1699 (0.097) |
| rung 3, untaught | 9 of 31 (0.290) | 4 of 38 (0.105) |

At rung 1 nothing is chosen yet in either and the two are the same reader. While taught at
rung 3, where about one trial in six outlasts the presses made at random, the reworked
reader was paid nearly twice as often. Its untaught test failed, below chance.

Why the test failed. After its last teaching the reworked reader gave the median unmarked
picture 0.65 and the median marked one 0.99. The network that says which steps to keep is
trusted only where the first is under half the second; it was not, so nothing was chosen,
and untaught, with no keeping at random, the slots stood empty at every question. The same
reader with that one figure set aside in scratch: 26 of 38 (0.684) on the test's seed and
22 of 39 (0.564) on another, 48 of 77 together. The package's reader of this run under the
reworked rule, nothing taught anew: 14 of 32 (0.438) against its own 9 of 31. So what was
learned under the reworked rule is the better reader, and the condition for trusting the
network is what let it down.

What gets marked. Of the pictures the two readers hold as tested, by whether they show the
red of the cue:

| | cue, marked | cue, unmarked | no cue, marked | no cue, unmarked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| package (175) | 46 | 12 | 95 | 22 |
| reworked (359) | 131 | 22 | 125 | 81 |

In both, pictures with no cue in them, the empty frames of the wait and the frame of the
last trial's outcome, are marked as often as the cue or more. In the reworked reader's
marks 0.86 of the cue's pictures are marked and 0.61 of the others, and a network fitted
to them can do no better than give those chances: enough to rank the two, which the
reworked rule needs, and not enough to put the others under a threshold of a half, which
the package's rule needs twice over (to choose a step, and to trust the network). The test that marks replaces every
place a picture holds in the row by the picture of now and marks it where the choice
changes. The empty frame of a trial fills up to eleven places of the buffer and a slot or
two; replaced in all of them at once the row is unlike any the reader was taught on, and
its choice changes for that reason.

Read on both points:

43. Yoon, Kim, Yang and Hwang 2018, Learning what to remember: long-term episodic memory
    networks (method and results in full, through a summary of the full text). A few slots
    over a long stream; a learned policy gives each held entry a retention value relative
    to the others and the entry let go is drawn by it, the incoming one among the
    candidates, so that a new entry can be refused. Trained by payment (A3C). Against
    first-in-first-out with five slots on bAbI: 17.5% errors against 41.4%; in mazes 73 to
    94% success against 60 to 91%. Taken: letting go by a value relative to what else is
    held, with refusal of the newcomer, is the known shape of the reworked rule, and
    first-in-first-out is their weak baseline. They have no threshold and no condition of
    trust; exploration is the drawing itself.
44. Sukhbaatar et al. 2021, Not all memories are created equal: learning to forget by
    expiring (method and experiments in full, through a summary of the full text). Each
    memory is given a learned span after which it is dropped; a memory dropped too early
    sends nothing back, and the paper has no means of recovering from that. Taken: a
    predictor of what to keep cannot correct itself on what it lets go, so keeping at
    random has to stay beside it; here that is the opening of chosen slots as often as
    judgements go unpaid, which makes the half-threshold on trust a second guard for the
    same danger.
45. Hooker, Mentch and Zhou 2019, Unrestricted permutation forces extrapolation (argument,
    simulations and alternatives, through a summary of the full text). Replacing a feature
    with a value that does not go with the others puts a flexible model where it has no
    data, and the change in its output is then credited to the feature; features that go
    together are overrated. Their remedy is to replace by a draw from what the feature
    could be given the rest. Taken: this is the marking of the empty frames. Tried
    offline, below.

## Theory and probe: why the reader sits at chance and then jumps, and what should end the wait

What is seen. Taught what a press is paid at once, a chance for each port, each picture
moved by itself, the reader stays at chance for forty to sixty rounds over eleven
thousand presses and then rises to 0.83 on trials kept apart within a few rounds. With
a third of the presses it never rises.

The theory. Whether a press is paid is a function of two things at once, where the
walker stands and where the goal stands, and of neither alone: knowing only one of them
says almost nothing of which port is paid. A function of that kind has no step for
descent to stand on. The gradient of the loss with respect to a feature of one thing
alone is the correlation of that feature with payment, which is near nought, so both
features must grow together out of noise before either is of use; the loss stays flat
until they do and then falls at once (Abbe, Boix-Adsera and Misiakiewicz, 2022 and
2023: descent learns a function fast where its parts can be learned one at a time, each
making the next visible, and needs samples and time that grow steeply with the size of
the input where a part needs two things at once, a leap). The cure the theory names is
a step: something learned first, from a signal that depends on one of the two things
alone, that makes the other visible to payment.

Such a signal is there on every press and costs nothing: which port was pressed, told
from the picture before and the picture after. It depends on the walker alone (what
moved, and which way), is told on every press that changed the frame, two bits each
where payment gives well under one and mostly noughts, and it names no task: it is what
the learner's own presses do. That what is learned from it is the part of the picture
the learner controls, and nothing else, is the theorem of Lamb et al. (2022) and Efroni
et al. (2021) on inverse models. With the walker held by the block, payment is a
function of one unknown thing given a known one, and its gradient is no longer nought.

Probe, on the bench: the same block and data, payment taught as before, with and without
a second part that is taught which port was pressed from the block's reading of the
picture before and after (both moved alike). Predicted: with it the score on first
presses kept apart passes 0.60 within 60 seconds; without it it is still below 0.40 at
60 seconds (seen once already: 0.293). If so, next: the same from a third of the
presses, where without it nothing is learned.

Result (two runs of 90 and 45 seconds, 18 and 9 rounds): the prediction failed and the
probe could not have told. The second part stayed at chance at its own task (the port
pressed told 0.23 to 0.27 of the time on pairs kept apart, with six places a side and
with twelve), so nothing was handed to payment (0.286 and 0.280 apart). Looking at the
pairs shows why: on this board a press is often stopped by a wall or the edge at once,
the walker stays, and only a pip goes out; from before and after no one could say which

## Theory and probe: payment read off what a press is foretold to do

The same theory, with a step that is there on every press. What a press does to the
picture is shown in full each time: the picture after it. That is taught with thousands
of numbers a press where payment gives one, it needs no payment at all, it is the same
kind of thing on every task (what my own press changes), and a blocked press is as
good a lesson as a slide: it shows where walls stop the walker.

And it changes what payment has to learn. As a function of the picture before the
press, payment needs the walker, the goal and what lies between them, points as far
apart as the board is wide: a function of several far things at once, with no step.
As a function of the picture the press is foretold to give, it needs one small thing:
the walker standing where the goal stood, one window of a few points anywhere in the
picture. A reading whose window is small has few weights and wants presses in
proportion to the window, not to the picture; and its gradient is not nought at the
start, since a walker laid on a goal is a pattern single windows can be judged by. So:

    chance(picture, port) = paid(picture, foretold(picture, port)),

with `foretold` taught on every press that did not end a trial and `paid` a few layers
of small windows and the largest of each map over the picture.

Probe on the bench (two minutes a run): a small network of halving and doubling layers
foretells the difference a press makes to the picture, taught 75 seconds on the pairs
of the trials not kept apart; then, held fixed, it feeds `paid`, taught 45 seconds on
what each press was paid at once. Predicted: (a) on pairs kept apart the foretold
picture is nearer the true one than the picture before is, its squared error under
half; (b) the score on first presses kept apart passes 0.60 within the 45 seconds,
where payment taught on the picture alone is at 0.29 after 60. If (a) fails the idea
is not tested; if (a) holds and (b) fails the theory is wrong.

Result so far. (a) failed as first built and holds in a second form. Taught the
difference of the pictures, point by point, the foretelling stayed worse than saying
"nothing changes" (squared error 1.17 times that, after twelve rounds and 200 seconds):
the known failure of such models on things a few points wide (Kaiser et al., paper 64
below). Taught instead where the picture changes, a map of one number a point with
the changed points weighted thirty times, it is right about the pip and the walker's
place at once and about where a slide ends more and more (overlap with the true map on
pairs kept apart 0.34 after six rounds, still rising; a blocked press is foretold to
change only the pip). (b) is not yet run as written, but a check of the bench on that
half-taught map gave, after one round of 24 seconds of payment, 0.494 on first presses
kept apart (0.442 on those taught): above anything the eye alone has shown before its
fortieth round. The run as predicted follows.

Result of the run as predicted (13:25 to 13:31, the graphics processor shared with two
helpers, so rounds were several times slower than alone). The map of where a press
changes the picture, taught 27 rounds in all on 6656 pairs: overlap with the true map
on pairs kept apart 0.69. Payment read off the picture and that map by a few layers of
small windows and the largest of each map, taught on the 11411 presses:

| round of payment | seconds | first presses taught | kept apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 21 | 0.532 | 0.535 |
| 2 | 47 | 0.959 | 0.921 |
| 3 | 78 | 0.970 | 0.954 |

(b) holds: 0.60 was passed within 47 seconds, and at three rounds the score apart is
0.954 where the eye alone reached 0.83 after sixty rounds or more and sat at chance
for the first forty. (a) holds in its second form only: the prediction was written for
the foretold picture, which failed; where the picture changes is what worked. The
foretelling itself took some six minutes shared; how much of it is needed, how few
presses both parts need, and whether the reading of small windows does it without
the map are the next runs.

## Probe: where to look, learned from payment (the tenth condition's clause, first attempt)

The window's rule is written by hand: the usual screen, the unusual points, their
middle. It names no task, but the goal as revised today asks that a learned rule be
attempted. The plainest learned form: a small network reads the whole coarse screen
and names a middle; the window, half the fine picture's side, is drawn from the fine
picture about that middle by a sampling that can be taught through (Jaderberg et al.,
2015, spatial transformers); the eye reads the window; all of it is taught by what a
press was paid at once, nothing else.

Theory, both ways. For: the middle is a function of one thing, where the board lies,
which on the coarse screen is a patch a few layers find; there is a step. Against:
payment's gradient reaches the middle only through the eye, and the eye says nothing
of payment until it has learned, which is what the window was needed for; each waits
for the other. What breaks the tie, if anything, is that the first window, at the
screen's middle, already holds most of most boards.

On the bench, all 11411 presses, 32 passes, the eye and score as before. Arms: the
middle learned; the window fixed at the screen's middle (what the learned arm begins
as). For reference, at 32 passes the whole screen gives 0.25 to 0.32 and the written
rule 0.997. Predicted: the fixed middle 0.50 to 0.80 (it sees most of most boards);
the learned middle above the fixed one by 0.10 or more and its middles spread out
from the screen's middle (a spread above 0.05 of the picture's half-side) by the
last pass. If the learned middles do not move, the tie was not broken.

Result (32 passes each, one seed):

| the window | first presses kept apart at pass 16, 24, 32 | spread of the middles |
|---|---|---|
| fixed at the screen's middle | 0.294, 0.308, 0.475 | none |
| its middle learned from payment | 0.322, 0.468, 0.614 | 0.000 at every pass from the twelfth |

The tie was not broken. The learned arm ends 0.14 above the fixed one, as predicted in
number, but not for the reason predicted: the network that names the middle gives the
same middle for every picture (a spread of 0.008 at the fourth pass, nought after),
so it does not follow the board; what differs between the arms is at most where one
fixed window lies and when the eye's jump comes, on one seed. The fixed window's own
prediction failed narrowly (0.475 where 0.50 to 0.80 was predicted). Worth keeping
from it: a window fixed at the screen's middle, with no rule at all, leaves chance by
the twenty-eighth pass, where the whole screen needs forty and more; less screen
helps even when it is not the right part.

So where to look is not learned from payment through the eye, at least not this
plainly: payment says nothing to the middle until the eye reads boards, as the theory
against had it. A signal that does not wait for payment is wanted (what the learner's
own presses change, or what it cannot foretell); a helper's reading on learned
looking is under way, and the next attempt waits for it.

## Probe: looking ahead through a foretold picture in place of levels above the first

Idea, from where the ladder breaks. A level above the first is taught a lesson
made of the levels below, and learns it slowly and from wrong marks. What it has to
find, though, is only what the press does to the picture; what that picture is
worth, level one already says. So teach one network the picture after a press, from
the picture and the port. Its lesson is the true picture after, given by every press
at every rung, paid or not, with no mark formed from another network's answers and
nothing that is the largest of several chances. Payment within k presses is then
asked by search (chapter 56, with what follows an action now learned): level one at
the picture foretold after one press, after two, and so on. Errors add only in use,
once for each press foretold, and can be measured press by press. Chapter 68 took a
map of where the picture changes for the same reason and found it needs thousands of
pairs; the stored presses now hold more than twenty thousand, and in the window the
board fills the picture.

On the bench: the helper's stored presses at rungs 2 and 3 with the window after
each press (about 23,000 pairs not paid at once, a fifth of the trials kept apart),
a network of halving and doubling layers taught the window after as the window plus
a difference, changed points weighing thirty times the rest; level one is the kept
reader's. Asked on trials kept apart, what a second level is taught: does level one
answer some port at the picture after? Predicted, after about eight minutes of
teaching on the shared processor: at the foretold picture level one answers 0.85 or
more of the pictures where it answers at the true one and 0.05 or less of the
others (the second level taught the same lesson answers 0.51 to 0.68 of right ports
and 0.15 to 0.17 of wrong ones on fresh trials); after two presses foretold one on
the other, 0.70 or more and 0.10 or less. No life if after one press it answers
under 0.6 of them.

Outcome: FAILED, no life. 1,946 batches of 64 in 235 s (the processor shared),
20,405 pairs taught, 2,000 presses of trials kept apart asked. The points that
change (0.0044 of the picture) are foretold to within 0.22 of what leaving the
picture as it was is off by; the rest to 0.0075. Level one answers some port at
0.318 of the true pictures after; at the foretold picture it answers 0.227 of those
(0.85 or more predicted) and 0.093 of the others; after two presses 0.202 and
0.109. Scored against the board on three fresh plays of the kept reader (rung 2 seed
9302, rung 3 seeds 9303 and 9306; the helper's measuring tool), a press chosen by
level one at the foretold pictures is on a shortest way 0.25, 0.25 and 0.16 of the
