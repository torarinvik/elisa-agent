# Research diary: 2 November 2026

Incremental finite-log overflow check: two required finite log weights of
`−10³⁰⁸` sum outside binary64 range. The cursor previously reported no
path; native and reference sweeps now report an arithmetic error. A failed
row update leaves the old row available for a subsequent valid update, and
a candidate with no possible suffix does not cause a scoring error. Positive
overflow is also rejected. In seed-250 full-width native forward sweeps,
median times over seven batches of ten calls were 0.009 versus 0.012 ms at
35 words and 0.127 versus 0.172 ms at 143, before and after checks. These
are local microbenchmarks, not end-to-end timings or rounding guarantees.

Prefix-reachability pruning: at position `k`, no reachable counter exceeds
`1 + k·max(A−1, 0)` for maximum arity `A`. Intersecting cursor rows with this
bound avoids scanning impossible states while retaining finite-log checks.
Seed-251 comparisons with fresh decoding after 12 row updates matched for
binary, ternary, and very large unused arities, with and without native
sweeps. Seed-252 full-width forward-sweep medians over seven batches were
0.011 versus 0.008 ms at 35 words, 0.164 versus 0.106 ms at 143, and 2.643
versus 1.678 ms at 575, before and after pruning. These are local native
sweep timings, not end-to-end performance guarantees.

Banded overflow check: the two-word unary-then-leaf sequence with both finite
log weights `−10³⁰⁸` previously returned no result; it now reports arithmetic
overflow. Finite log-tail arithmetic overflow is likewise distinguished from
an absent tail. In-band transitions are restricted to their actual target
interval, and inputs safely inside a per-path magnitude budget use an
unchecked fast path. Seed-253 exhaustive small-band comparisons matched
selected scores and total weights. For seed-254 width-seven passes, medians
over seven batches of ten calls were 0.304 versus 0.229 ms at 35 words and
1.451 versus 0.921 ms at 143 before and after the changes. These Python-path
microbenchmarks are local and do not establish outward-rounded risk bounds.

Native one-shot band pass: the sum and maximum recurrences use two rolling
rows and retain choices for traceback. Seed-255 random bands over binary,
ternary, and very large unused arities agreed with the NumPy fallback, and
seed-253 small bands agreed with enumeration. The existing extreme-overflow
cases remain covered. On seed-256 width-seven inputs, median per-pass times
over seven batches were 0.230 versus 0.014 ms at 35 words, 0.955 versus
0.055 ms at 143, and 3.904 versus 0.326 ms at 575, for NumPy and Elisa
respectively. These include the Python wrapper and are local microbenchmarks,
not end-to-end latency or general numerical-error guarantees.

Banded endpoint check: a band excluding final counter zero cannot contain an
accepted path. Previously the native pass returned no path immediately, but
the NumPy pass could overflow while evaluating an irrelevant intermediate
row. Both now stop at the endpoint check. Seed-258 sparse tied inputs across
2,700 cases, including 1,723 accepted cases, matched between the native and
reference passes. Tests also cover signed 64-bit extreme NumPy band centres,
which are converted before arithmetic to avoid integer overflow. These are
finite comparisons, not a floating-point error bound.

General prefix parser and input check: fractional arity `1.5` was silently
truncated to `1` by the exact and banded decoders; malformed alphabets and
row matrices are now rejected before native calls. The general parser now
uses an iterative stack, including for a 1,500-level prefix, and the Elisa
version matched the reference over all arity sequences of lengths 1–6 with
arities 0–3. Lean verifies that any successful counter run conserves pending
slots and that a complete sequence has `m−1` child edges. Seven-batch local
parser medians for 1,504 words were 0.383 ms in Python versus 0.327 ms in
Elisa, including Python output conversion; these are not end-to-end timings.

Prior alphabet and selection check: with alphabet `(0, 1, 3)`, a selected
symbol `1.5` was truncated to `1` by native format risk, producing zero risk,
while the reference treated it as a different symbol. Risk and prior APIs now
reject noninteger selections before conversion. The prior automaton no longer
restricts its integer arities to 0–2: native and reference computations for
a ternary prefix automaton matched the format decoder's normalizer, maximum
weight, and marginals. A local 575-symbol prior-weight microbenchmark rose
from 0.0070 ms before validation to 0.0146 ms after vectorized type checking;
these are per-call timings, not end-to-end results.

Prior label range check: a prior alphabet containing `10¹⁰⁰` decoded with the
reference implementation but failed native conversion to signed 64-bit.
The prior now relabels out-of-range symbols by dense column index for native
calls and restores original labels on output. A seed-260 four-state prior
matched an explicitly relabelled automaton on selected path, normalizer,
marginals, and selected/alternative partition, with and without native
kernels. Lean proves preservation of a path's prior weight when relabelling
has a left inverse on that path. In a separate ordinary-label microbenchmark,
575-symbol prior-weight calls had medians 0.0147 ms before and 0.0162 ms
after over seven 500-call batches. The separate format decoder still has a
signed 64-bit native alphabet restriction.

Format arity range check: an alphabet containing `10¹⁰⁰` previously failed
native conversion although that arity cannot fit a four-word prefix. For a
fixed `m`-word input, every arity at least `m` is now mapped to the same
unreachable native sentinel; original labels remain in the public interface.
Lean proves every arity in an accepted sequence is less than `m`. Exact,
banded, risk, and incremental results matched their references for an alphabet
with two distinct huge unused arities, including a valid path of log weight
−1000. Seed-261 ordinary three-arity exact-decode medians over seven batches
of 50 calls were 0.0124 versus 0.0130 ms at 35 words and 0.0879 versus
0.0869 ms at 143, before and after; these local timings include noise.

Finite tail support: for `m` words no sequence can differ from a reference
in more than `m` positions, so the tail at `m+1` changes is exactly zero.
Lean verifies the difference-count and empty-tail statements. For uniform
three-arity rows, the former polynomial search selected 1,047 changes at
575 words and 18,126 at 10,000; the finite-support search stops at 576 and
10,001. With the search in Elisa and a Python crossing check for `lgamma`
rounding differences, median complete `band_changes` times over seven
batches fell from 0.176 to 0.072 ms at 575 words and from 3.018 to 1.172 ms
at 10,000. A 2,202-case boundary sweep matched the reference search; these
local timings and comparisons are not end-to-end or outward-rounding proofs.

Tail-reference input check: `band_changes` previously accepted fractional
column indices such as `0.5` and silently converted them to `0`. It now
requires one in-range integer per row, including for both native and reference
execution. Integer arrays are checked with vector operations rather than a
Python per-element loop. For uniform three-column rows, seven-batch median
`band_changes` timings were 0.071 versus 0.038 ms at 575 rows and 1.160 versus
0.562 ms at 10,000 before and after; these local measurements are not
end-to-end performance claims.
