## Contradiction screening before acquisition (§20.16)

Prediction, before running. A 64-case prefix should expose enough disjoint
contradictory pairs to reject most one-input contracts in the combining
experiment before their 4,096-case fit. The separate false-discard budget is
0.05, spent as η/((k+1)(k+2)); admission retains its own 0.05 budget and fresh
test. With tolerance 0.05, the exact threshold solves
`binom(64,r) 0.05^r ≤ η_k`. Deterministic two-input targets have no
contradictions, so screening should preserve service and evaluated accuracy.
I predict at least a tenfold reduction in unary labels and at least a
twofold reduction in binary labels, fewer fits and lower CPU time.

The benchmark now uses separate random streams for codebooks, teaching cases
and tasks. Both policies therefore receive identical task sequences; teaching
streams diverge when consumption differs. Baselines are rerun rather than
compared directly with the earlier shared-stream numbers. Requests now count
all acquisition attempts, including those on unserved tasks; the former
served-task-only count remains separately named. Report means and sample
standard deviations over seeds 3726–3728. No persistent refusals are stored.

Measured results, mean ± sample standard deviation; labels, requests and fits
are per task, CPU seconds cover each complete 20-task run:

| Policy | Served | Accuracy | Labels | Requests | Fits | CPU seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unary | 0 | — | 24,582.7 ± 0.2 | 6.00 ± 0.00 | 6.00 ± 0.00 | 0.3202 ± 0.0079 |
| Unary + screen | 0 | — | 384.0 ± 0.0 | 6.00 ± 0.00 | 0.00 ± 0.00 | 0.0140 ± 0.0002 |
| Binary | 1 | 1 | 11,333.7 ± 410.4 | 2.75 ± 0.10 | 2.75 ± 0.10 | 0.2819 ± 0.0201 |
| Binary + screen | 1 | 1 | 1,654.1 ± 7.1 | 2.75 ± 0.10 | 0.35 ± 0.00 | 0.1586 ± 0.0170 |

Both label predictions held: reductions were 64.0× unary and 6.85× binary.
CPU fell 22.8× and 1.78× respectively. Every unary request was screened out;
binary screened 2.40 ± 0.10 requests per task and fitted only the 0.35
admissions. No evaluated deterministic binary target was falsely discarded.
These three seeds do not establish the statistical guarantee: that bound
depends on fresh sampling, deterministic key semantics and sound label
separation. The finite tests also enumerate all six-case samples over two
inputs and two labels against all four predictors, and exactly sum one
eight-case IID false-discard experiment rather than estimating its tail.

Requests are unchanged. The remaining repeated refusals suggest scoped
contract/refusal memory, with distribution and agreement semantics and a
durable spending counter; a statistical refusal must not become an unscoped
permanent ban. Sparse or continuous input keys may yield no collisions, so
this screen gives no universal reduction in label cost.

## Durable append positions under eviction (§43.12)

Prediction, before running. On a workload that appends one record, scans after
the saved cursor, evicts the record and reopens, the previous allocation rule
`max(live positions)+1` repeats position 1. Its model therefore exposes only
the first of 100 or 1,000 admissions. A transactional durable high-water mark
should expose every admission, end at cursor 100 or 1,000 and spend no labels
or expert requests. Measure elapsed and CPU seconds including reopening on
every cycle. This is a correctness experiment, not a claimed speedup. The
legacy comparator is an explicit model, not a separately timed old binary.

Measured results:

| Admissions | Visible after eviction/reopen | Legacy model visible | Last cursor | Elapsed seconds | CPU seconds |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 100 | 1 | 100 | 0.0575 | 0.0517 |
| 1,000 | 1,000 | 1 | 1,000 | 0.5320 | 0.4736 |

The prediction held exactly; labels and expert requests were zero. This
single-run timing describes overhead, not a speed comparison or a durability
proof. Exhaustive four-operation traces additionally cover duplicate appends
and removal of the newest live record. These observations establish the
cursor prerequisite only: scoped refusal memory and persistent confidence
allocation remain unimplemented. Schema-1 migration requires an external
upper bound on deleted positions, an explicitly trusted input.

## Scoped refusal memory and durable spending (§20.17)

Prediction, before running. Reusing statistically established refusals in the
same versioned context should lower repeated unary requests and labels in the
combining experiment. Screened unary currently uses six new requests and 384
labels per task. I predict at least a twofold reduction in both with refusal
memory; binary should retain service and evaluated accuracy 1 while reducing
labels further. Durable positions include history records and skipped levels,
so admission tests may need more fresh labels than their local-counter
equivalents. CPU may increase because every new reservation is durable; no
speedup is predicted. Reuse the three seeds 3726–3728 and separate task and
sampling streams. Report actual new acquisition requests separately from
cached callback requests; planner callback attempts may remain unchanged.
No failed fit or validation test is persisted as an impossibility claim.

Measured results, mean ± sample standard deviation over the three seeds.
Requests, cache hits and labels are per task; CPU seconds cover 20 tasks:

| Policy | New requests | Cached requests | Labels | CPU seconds | Served | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unary + screen | 6.00 ± 0.00 | 0 | 384.0 ± 0.0 | 0.01386 ± 0.00005 | 0 | — |
| Unary + memory | 1.80 ± 0.00 | 4.20 ± 0.00 | 115.2 ± 0.0 | 0.01835 ± 0.00024 | 0 | — |
| Binary + screen | 2.75 ± 0.10 | 0 | 1,654.1 ± 7.1 | 0.15818 ± 0.02483 | 1 | 1 |
| Binary + memory | 2.08 ± 0.10 | 0.67 ± 0.08 | 1,617.8 ± 6.7 | 0.17397 ± 0.01723 | 1 | 1 |

The unary prediction held: new attempts and labels fell 3.33×. Binary labels
fell only 2.2%; its remaining cost is mostly training and certifying admitted
experts, and skipped durable positions make certification more conservative.
No accuracy or service loss was observed. CPU increased 32% unary and 10%
binary, consistent with the predicted possibility of durable-write overhead;
these small timings are not a general throughput result. Timings exclude
initial database creation but include task-time durable writes. Planner callback
attempts are unchanged: the history prevents repeat sampling and fitting after
the callback, rather than removing those candidates from type-driven search.

Restart tests reuse a stored refusal with zero new labels, confirm that a
changed context retries at a smaller spending level, and interrupt sampling
after reservation to verify that reopening cannot reuse its level. Bounded
index misses and eviction trigger new reservations; neither resets the clock.
The remaining learning bottleneck is admitted experts' large fixed training
budget. Semantic candidate selection and planner-level avoidance of known
refusals remain separate open mechanisms.
