# Research diary, 16 November 2026

## Probe: sokoban by learned consequences and search, now ahead of random presses (development, scratch only)

Carried on from yesterday's probe. Four changes made it work.

The press that ends a puzzle was not being kept as a press, since it comes with a payment, so the player
had never seen what pushing a crate onto the pad does and no search could end. Keeping it mended that.

The four ports are one rule turned four ways. Each port's cases are now tried against every other port's
table under the eight turnings of the grid, and those that agree on every case they share are pooled.
After pooling the four ports hold one table of 486 to 600 cases, each over the two cells either side
along its own way and what the cell began with.

Marking as dead ends the surroundings of a crate met where a puzzle ended unpaid was dropped: four
neighbours do not say whether a crate can still reach the pad, and it set aside pushes that were needed.
With consequences foretold rightly the search does not walk into such a place, because no plan leads out
of it.

A plan once found is followed while the frames go as foretold, and where there is none the player walks
without pushing for thirty presses before it presses at random again.

Two runs of 200 seconds at the first rung, the first 800 presses at random, later half of the puzzles:

| seed | puzzles | planned puzzles paid above nought | unplanned puzzles paid above nought |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 37 | 6 of 6 | 9 of 31 |
| 3 | 32 | 9 of 11 | 6 of 21 |

A puzzle planned from its first press is solved in four to six presses with the fewest pushes, which is
what the task pays above nought. A plan is found for a third to a half of the later puzzles; for the
rest some neighbourhood on the way has not been seen and the player refuses to foretell it (46.6). It is
slow, about seven presses a second.

Tuned on these seeds; not evidence, and not packaged.

## The player of several presses packaged, and fresh seeds of the task it was built on

The probe is now `worlds.py` in `nca-choices` (commit `df5c064`), with what was added after the entry
above. The board is taken anew from each puzzle's first frame, its cells as wide as the empty lines
across it are apart. Kinds are told by sixteen parts of a cell, not its mean. What a cell held when the
puzzle began is part of every case, so a pad under the worker is not lost. Where no plan is found the
player goes to the nearest press it cannot foretell instead of walking aimlessly. What a plan counts,
presses or only presses that change more cells than usual, is whichever has been paid more often.

On the maze of the development set (seed 2, 220 s) one maze was solved by plan in the fewest presses
and two planned mazes were not paid; the player stalls on the sixth maze, a larger board on which kinds
appear that it has never pressed against. That is not mended.

seeds 5 and 6 were run once, after the commit, and nothing was changed for them:

## Preregistration: the player of several presses on the four held-out tasks

The four held-out reasoning tasks that take several presses (`video_sequence`, `sudoku`,
`crossed_wires`, `chess`) have been run once by the one-press players and never looked at. `worlds.play`
at commit `df5c064` is run on each, unchanged, first rung, seed 11, 280 seconds, one run each.

A task passes if at least five of its later puzzles were planned from their first press, at least 60% of
those were paid above nought, and that share is above the share of all unplanned puzzles paid above
nought. A task that passes is run once more on seed 12 under the same bar before anything is claimed.

Prediction: none passes. The player was built on one task where a worker moves on a grid; it assumes a
board of square cells and a goal that is the absence of a kind. Two of the four are probably not drawn
on such a board, and on the other two a press probably depends on more than five nearby cells.

## The player that arranges tiles, packaged (development)

The arrangement probe of 15 November, put aside then, is now `arranged.py` in `nca-choices` (commit
`d35bd82`). A port that draws a thin mark round a part of the frame and takes it away when pressed again
names a place, and pressing two such ports exchanges what they hold. An arrangement is sought by a
picture shown elsewhere in the frame, by edges that run on into their neighbours (read at four looks,
since tiles may move), or, newly, by each tile being most like the one before it along the places,
forwards or backwards. The way used is the one the task has paid most often. The ways are written by
hand; only the choice among them is learned, from payment.

Development tasks, first rung, seeds not used before, each run once:

| task | seed | seconds | puzzles | paid above nought |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| jigsaw | 9 | 40 | 20 | 20 |
| jigsaw | 10 | 90 | 48 | 48 |
| video jigsaw | 9 | 60 | 15 | 3 |
| video jigsaw | 10 | 60 | 17 | 12 |
| video jigsaw | 11 | 60 | 17 | 13 |
| video jigsaw | 12 | 60 | 11 | 4 |

The two jigsaw runs were made before seams were read at four looks, which does not touch the way they
used. Video jigsaw is uneven, 32 of 60 over four seeds, and runs of the same seed differ; why is not
known. Random presses were paid on neither task (15 November).

## Preregistration: the arranging player on the held-out task `video_sequence`

`video_sequence` is held out and has not been looked at. What is known of it: its name, the first lines
of its description read before the split, that the one-press players were paid nothing on it, and that
under the player of several presses, pressing mostly at random, 4 of 26 puzzles were paid above nought
(15%). Whether its ports mark places, whether pressing two exchanges them, and what it shows are not
known.

`arranged.play` at `d35bd82`, unchanged, first rung, 200 seconds, seed 21 and then seed 22, one run
each. A run passes if at least ten puzzles were arranged and at least 40% of them were paid above
nought. The claim needs both runs to pass. Seed 22 is run only if seed 21 passes.

Prediction: more likely to fail than pass, about one in three. It passes only if the task's ports mark
places as the development tasks' do and if the right order is the one in which neighbours are most
alike.

## Result: the arranging player on `video_sequence`, failed

Run as preregistered (`arranged.play` at `d35bd82`, first rung, seed 21, 200 seconds, once):

| puzzles arranged | paid above nought | by seams | by likeness | by likeness backwards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 79 | 1 | 0 of 20 | 1 of 40 | 0 of 19 |

Failed: 1 of 79 against a bar of 40%. Seed 22 was not run. Four ports were found to mark places, so that
part of what the player assumes held. But its arrangements were paid less often than presses made mostly
at random had been (4 of 26), so what it does after finding the places is worse than doing nothing in
particular: either pressing two places does not exchange them there, or the order wanted is not the one
in which neighbours are most alike. Which, is not known, and the task has still not been looked at.

Both players written today assume how a task is worked, pushing on a grid or exchanging tiles, and each
holds on unseen puzzles of the tasks it was built on and fails on tasks it was not. Neither learns how a
task is worked from its frames.
