# Research diary, 5 November 2026

## COGS edit-rule baseline: preregistration

External package `nca-cogs` pins train and dev from upstream revision
`165a7b6`; `test.tsv` and `gen.tsv` are not downloaded. The task is the whole
sentence-to-logical-form mapping. Fit the unchanged `fit_edit_rules` on all 24,155
training rows and gate on a hash partition of dev (tolerance/confidence 0.05),
auditing only if certified. Diagnostic: fit on nine tenths of training sentences
and score the remaining unseen ones. Dev labels are not used for it.

This tests the learner's bias outside the grammar it happened to fit on SCAN.
Predict 0/1 certified, held-out exact match at most 20%, most held-out sentences
refused, and fewer than 30 rules. Logical forms use variable indices tied to token
position, which single-token edits with list operations cannot shift. One run
on the vast box, 12-core machine, single process, 295-second limit.

### COGS edit-rule baseline result

| Certified | Gate labels | Rules | Atoms | Held-out exact | Refused | CPU |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0/1 | 1 | 0 | 24,155 | 0/2,378 | 2,378 | 5.9 s |

The predictions held. The learner reduces to a lookup table of every training row,
so the 100% SCAN result does not transfer: it reflected that grammar's fit
to one-token edits. COGS needs what this space lacks: sub-parts that are not
standalone training sentences, outputs whose variable indices depend on token
position, and word classes that generalize (a noun's rule applying to other nouns).
No test or generalization data were downloaded.
Evidence: `nca-cogs/evidence/edit-rules-baseline.json`.

## COGS template learner: preregistration

The structural package adds `fit_template_rules`. It learns a lexical output
token per word, rewrites outputs into slots for lexical tokens and decimal position tokens,
groups words by the tokens following their lexical token, and maps each sequence of
classes to its most frequent template. There is no recursion; unseen shapes are refused.
Same data and partition as the baseline. Predict held-out exact match of
40–85%, with most errors being refusals, and 0/1 certified, because refusals alone
should exceed the 5% tolerance. A negative gate is the expected result; it would
show word classes help without solving composition. `test.tsv` and `gen.tsv` stay
undownloaded.

### COGS template learner result

| Certified | Gate labels | Held-out exact | Refused | Wrong | CPU |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0/1 | 31 | 2,074/2,378 (87.2%) | 253 (10.6%) | 51 (2.1%) | 2.7 s |

Certification failed as predicted, and refusals outnumber wrong answers about 5:1.
Held-out accuracy beat the predicted 40–85%. This held-out set has the
training distribution; it says nothing about COGS's generalization set, which
tests recursion depth and words in new roles, and a template table without
recursion should fail most of the depth cases. The gate needs at most 5% error, so
the next step is composition for unseen shapes, not more lexical work.
Evidence: `nca-cogs/evidence/templates-development.json`.

## Sort-by decomposition: preregistration

Of 140 unserved seed-one passive families, 59 contain order and 50 filter. The
most common shapes are order over an input (27 occurrences) and order or filter over traversal
(20 and 12). Add to the standard package a generic `sort-by` expert with a
`(a, a) → flag` slot. Its proposal-only witness requires the output to be a
permutation of the proposed source, and derives comparator examples from adjacent
output pairs: earlier-before-later true, the reverse false, skipping equal items. Extend
witness argument hints with ground slot-free experts applied to inputs, which makes
learned unary abbreviations available as sources. Existing soundness and the fresh
whole-route gate are unchanged.

Run the control arms on the vast box, compared with the recorded v2 controls
(seed 1: 65 queried, 60 passive; seed 2: 61, 54). Predict 3–15 gains per arm, at most 2
losses, and largest served size at most 46. If an arm hits the time limit, report
it as incomplete. Sealed sets stay sealed.

### Sort-by decomposition result: rejected

With the original witness order, seed-one passive went 60 → 58: no gains, losing
D0029 and D0122, because the `sort-by` branch was never reached before the
expansion limit. An unregistered amendment ordered witnesses by fewest free type
variables. Seed one then went 60 → 58 passive (6 gains, 8 losses) and 65 → 61 queried
(6 gains, 10 losses, including D0010). The gains were the plain sort families D0008,
D0062, D0082, D0089, D0094 and D0132. Zero-branching concatenation consumes the
expansions that other families need. Seed-two arms were stopped; the prediction
of at most 2 losses failed. Both the ordering and the expert were reverted; the
active state is the v2 control. Sort needs a search that shares budget fairly
across branches, not another witness.

## Learner portfolio across domains: preregistration

Until now a person chose the learner per benchmark. Add a generic core `portfolio`
fitter. It fits each supplied learner on four fifths of the training examples,
scores them on the remaining fifth, refits the best on all examples, and passes one
frozen candidate to `CertifiedAcquirer`. Selection reads no fresh cases, so
`acquisition_sound` applies unchanged. One learner set (edit rules, templates,
bounded route search) runs unchanged on every task, with only data, contract and type
universe supplied per domain.

Predict: SCAN chooses a rule learner, certifies 1/1, audit at least 124/128.
COGS chooses templates, 0/1 certified. Passive D-50 chooses search on every family
that any learner fits, 8–13/50 certified (per-family confidence 0.001; fitting
twice and a smaller selection split may lose families against the
direct 13/50). All runs on the vast box under 295 seconds per invocation. Upstream
SCAN test results are not reused; COGS `gen`/`test` and C0004–8 remain unopened.

All predictions held; D-50 is at the top of the predicted range and equals the

## COGS span insertion: preregistration

Of 304 held-out template failures, 218 are unseen shapes reducible to a known
shape by removing one contiguous span; 51 wrong, 25 other shapes, 10 unknown words.
Extend `fit_template_rules` with learned span insertions: compare known shapes
differing by a span (at most 6 words, support at least 2), shift positions, and
record inserted output chunks with their preceding context relative to the span.
An unseen shape reduces recursively and re-inserts chunks at the best-matching
gap, with ties resolved by increasing position. No COGS token or word class is named.

Run through the unchanged portfolio. For sequence tasks, gate with the core's
fixed-size positive-error test (`validation_cases` 1,000, §20.22, chosen now, drawn
with replacement from the gate part) because an accurate but imperfect mapping
would otherwise nearly always fail a zero-error test; the tolerance stays 5%.
Predict: held-out exact match 92–98%, SCAN still chooses a learner and certifies,
D-50 unchanged at 13/50. COGS certification is borderline; predict 0/1 because
known failures alone are near the permitted error count. This remains the
training distribution. `gen.tsv` and `test.tsv` stay undownloaded.

### COGS span insertion result: rejected

| Held-out (2,378) | Before | With spans |
|---|---:|---:|
| Exact | 2,074 (87.2%) | 2,157 (90.7%) |
| Wrong | 51 | 156 |
| Refused | 253 | 65 |

The accuracy prediction (92–98%) failed. Spans fixed 83 sentences and turned 105
refusals into wrong answers. Inserted chunks treat the logical form as an ordered token
list; it is really a sorted definite prefix and a set of conjuncts, so chunks land out
of order, and a second nested phrase attaches to the wrong head. Span insertion was
reverted because wrong answers are worse than refusals.

Separately, a refused input aborted the gate, which had invalidated the COGS gate results
above. Refusals now count as gate errors. Rerun with the fixed harness and without spans:
COGS 0/1 (chosen templates, stopped at 511 of 1,000 gate labels once errors exceeded
the allowance), SCAN 1/1 (1,000 gate labels, audit 128/128), D-50 13/50. The next attempt
should learn output structure (a prefix and an unordered conjunct set) before composing.
`gen.tsv` and `test.tsv` remain undownloaded.

## COGS structured span insertion: preregistration

Second attempt. Output separators are selected from examples by greedy compression of
the distinct delexicalized segments. Span effects become segments with their separator
group; composition inserts whole segments, by increasing position within a
terminated group and otherwise next to the segment sharing a slot; the rightmost span is
removed first. Measure on held-out training sentences first (baseline 2,074 exact,
51 wrong, 253 refused). Predict separators *AND* and *;*, exact 90–97%, wrong at most
120. Acceptance rule, fixed now: keep only if newly correct sentences outnumber newly
wrong ones; otherwise revert. If kept, run the portfolio on all three domains. Predict
COGS 0/1 certified unless held-out accuracy reaches about 97%. `gen.tsv` and `test.tsv`
stay undownloaded.

## COGS structured span insertion: result

Rejected and reverted. Held-out training sentences, 2,378; baseline 2,074 exact, 51 wrong,
253 refused.

| Variant | Separators | Exact | Wrong | Refused |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Preregistered cost (dictionary only) | 12 tokens | 2,074 | 245 | 59 |
| Cost with segment references | `,` | 2,076 | 100 | 202 |
| Diagnostic, separators supplied by hand | *AND*, *;* | 2,162 | 129 | 87 |

Both learned variants fail the acceptance rule (0 and 2 newly correct against 194 and 49
newly wrong). The separator prediction failed in both. The cost correction was made after
seeing the first measurement, so it is a post-hoc variant. The third row cannot be accepted
because its tokens were supplied by hand and it was run only to separate selector faults
from composition faults; its exact count of 90.9% is at the bottom of the predicted range

Two faults remain. Greedy selection stops at a local optimum that misses the true
separators. With them supplied, wrong sentences still show misordered conjuncts and wrong
heads for nested modifiers. The portfolio was not run, as its run depended on acceptance.

## COGS learned part order: preregistration

Third composition attempt; its design was informed by errors seen on this held-out split,
so the split is no longer untouched for this question. A token becomes a separator when
nearly every piece it cuts contains a position slot, replacing compression. The last separator
of most templates joins the body; the others terminate header parts. If adjacent parts of
training templates are mostly in increasing first-position order, insertion keeps each group
sorted, stably. Predict separators *AND* and *;*, exact 2,200–2,300 of 2,378, wrong at most
100. Acceptance rule unchanged: newly correct must outnumber newly wrong against the baseline
(2,074 exact, 51 wrong, 253 refused). Wrong-head errors are not addressed and should remain.
If kept, run the portfolio on all three domains; predict COGS stays 0/1 unless held-out
accuracy reaches about 97%.

## COGS learned part order: result

Rejected and reverted. Held-out training sentences, 2,378; baseline 2,074 exact, 51 wrong,
253 refused.

| Variant | Separators | Exact | Wrong | Refused |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Preregistered, learned | *AND*, *;*, *theme*, *nmod*, *xcomp* | 2,083 | 208 | 87 |
| Diagnostic, supplied by hand | *AND*, *;* | 2,199 | 92 | 87 |

The learned variant adds 9 correct and 157 wrong, failing the rule, and its separator and

the baseline 97.6%. Remaining sampled errors point a clause argument at the wrong position
or attach a nested modifier to the wrong head. The portfolio was not run.

## COGS derivation agreement: preregistration

Hypothesis: wrong heads and misdirected clause arguments arise because composition takes the
first reduction found when others disagree. Change: enumerate every reduction, memoized by
shape, and answer only if all derivable results agree; disagreement is a refusal. Measured with
*AND* and *;* supplied by hand, so this is a diagnostic of composition and cannot be
adopted until separators are learned. Reference: 2,199 exact, 92 wrong, 87 refused of 2,378.
Predict wrong at most 45, exact at least 2,100. Supported only if wrong falls by more than exact
falls. Then fit-time and predict-time feasibility must stay within the five-minute cap.

## COGS derivation agreement: result

Not supported; scaffold removed. With *AND* and *;* supplied by hand: 2,171 exact, 72 wrong, 135
refused, against 2,199, 92 and 87. Exact fell by 28 and wrong by only 20, and the predictions
(wrong at most 45, exact at least 2,100) were met only for exact. Precision is 96.8%.
Prediction takes about five seconds in total, so cost is no constraint.

The remaining sampled errors have agreeing derivations, which contradicts the hypothesis
for those cases. In *said that a giraffe wanted to walk*, the prediction keeps
*say . ccomp* on *walk* instead of retargeting it to *want*; the nested modifier sample
behaves likewise. Inserted spans appear to take over a reference held by an existing part, which
purely additive effects cannot express. This is drawn from a sample and has not been counted.

## COGS reference retargeting: preregistration

Span effects additionally learn rewrites: a part of the shorter example, absent from the longer,
that matches a new part except for one position slot. Rewrites are voted separately per
span and apply only to parts of matching shape holding the old position. Diagnostic with *AND* and
*;* supplied by hand, ordered, without derivation agreement; not adoptable until separators are
learned. Reference: 2,199 exact, 92 wrong, 87 refused of 2,378. First count reference errors
differing from the truth in exactly one position token; predict at least half of the 92. Predict
exact at least 2,220 and wrong at most 60. Supported only if newly correct outnumber newly wrong
against the reference.

## COGS reference retargeting: result

Weakly supported as a diagnostic; not adopted. With *AND* and *;* supplied by hand: 2,213 exact,
99 wrong, 66 refused, against 2,199, 92 and 87. Exact rose by 14 and wrong by 7, meeting the rule,
but both accuracy predictions failed (at least 2,220 exact, at most 60 wrong). Only 12 of the 92
reference errors differ from the truth in one position token, against a prediction of at least half;
78 differ in other tokens, so pointer errors are not the main remaining fault. Precision falls
from 96.0% to 95.7%. The scaffold has no separator learner and is not in the package.

## COGS behavioural word classes: preregistration

A word's signature becomes the set of its per-example context sets, so a word's behaviour in one
sentence stays together, instead of one pooled set of contexts. A word whose signature is a strict
subset of a larger group's joins the largest such group. Measured without separators or
composition. Baseline of 2,378 held-out sentences: 2,074 exact, 51 wrong, 253 refused. Predict
wrong at most 25, exact at least 2,090, refused at most 270. Keep only if the exact gain exceeds the
wrong gain and the package tests pass. Words seen only in frames shared by two groups stay
under-determined, so some errors should remain. If kept, run the portfolio on all three domains;
predict COGS stays 0/1.

## COGS behavioural word classes: result

Kept. Held-out sentences, 2,378: 2,174 exact, 85 wrong, 119 refused, against 2,074, 51 and 253. Exact
rose by 100 and wrong by 34, passing the rule; accuracy is 91.4%, up from 87.2%, and precision 96.2%,
down from 97.6%. The exact and refusal predictions held; the wrong prediction (at most 25) failed.
Role errors were not removed. Sampled wrong answers still swap roles, now also giving *theme* to
*sleep*, *scream* and *giggle*, so the gain came chiefly from fewer refusals, and joining a word to the
largest containing group appears to over-merge. Portfolio on the box: SCAN 1/1, COGS 0/1, procedural
D-50 13/50, each unchanged in service.
