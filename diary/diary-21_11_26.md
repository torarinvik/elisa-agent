## Test stated before its run: exploring by how new the picture is to a port

Last night's draw by the reader's shares kept the waits and never pressed at
the question. The draw is now made to turn on what the shares cannot tell:
how often a press of that port has been judged at a picture like this one.
The picture is made small (12 by 12, its mean taken off, of unit length) and
for each port a ridge of one is kept over the pictures at which a press of it
was judged; the width of the picture of now against that ridge is 1 where no
like picture was met and 1 / (1 + n) where the same was met n times (Zahavy et
al., 2018, with the picture itself for the features). When a press is to be
made at random each port is drawn with its width for weight and pressing
nothing with weight one. At a picture where every port has been judged often
the draw is nearly always to wait; at one that is new to a port it is as even
as it was. A written rule, said plainly: the small picture and the ridge of
one are chosen by hand; features the reader learns are the replacement to be
tried.

Test: `newest_counts`, rung 1, the memory learner (package `47940d5`), 3,000
presses from nothing on the map's seeds. Prediction: after the first teaching
a trial lasts 30 presses or more (12 with even draws, 20 by shares); 0.10 or
more of the judgements after the first play are paid (0.012 to 0.036, and
none); of the draws made, one in twenty or more is a port. It fails if the
picture at the question is no newer to the ridge than the pictures of the
wait.

## Test stated before its run: lessons from the frame after where the reading is learnable

Yesterday's test of these lessons was on `which_moved`, where the teller was
right and the reader learned nothing with or without them, so it said nothing
of what they are worth to a reader that can learn. Idea, unchanged: a reader
of one picture, taught by payment alone on the frame after a judged press
less the frame at it, names the paid port; on an unpaid judged press the port
it names is taken as a paid lesson. Theory: with k ports pressed evenly a
judged press is paid one time in k, so n judged presses give n/k lessons that
name the port and n(1 - 1/k) that only strike one out; where the teller is
trusted on a share c of the unpaid, the lessons that name the port become
n(1/k + c(1 - 1/k)), for k = 4 and c = 0.85 some 3.5 times as many, and a
reader that learns should stand, at n judged presses, about where it stood at
3n without. The teller is wrong on a lesson at most as often as it is wrong on
unpaid presses, which payment never shows; that is the assumption measured
below on paid ones.

Run: stored random play, 6,000 steps each (a port or nothing at every step,
evenly), of `changed_tile`, `which_is_new`, `next_in_turn` and `shape_back`,
at rung 1; the memory learner's reading (reach 4, no slots) taught on the
judged presses of the first four fifths and asked on the rest, with and
without the lessons, the teller taught on the same four fifths. Predictions:
(a) on at least three of the four the teller's two parts each name the paid
port on 0.9 of the paid presses kept apart; (b) on at least two, the reader
with the lessons names the paid port on the paid presses kept apart 0.15 more
often than the reader without. Where (a) fails for a task the frame after
does not show the answer there, and the lessons are silent.
