# Research diary, 19 November 2026

## Probe: three readers up short_maze on the same seeds, one with a count of presses to payment (development)

Read first, since yesterday's worth did not tell progress. Rank2Reward (Yang et al. 2024,
method section in full): progress is learned by ranking the frames within successful
trajectories by time; off those trajectories the ranking gives spurious high values, which
the paper damps with a classifier of the successes' states against the policy's own. SASR
(Ma et al. 2024, method section in full): a state is scored by the share of its occurrences
in successful trajectories, which is what yesterday's worth was, and the paper names the same
limit, that it ignores where in a trajectory a state lies. Taken: order within the paid
trials, not membership of them, as in Paul et al. read yesterday. Not taken: the classifier
against states never paid.

The count: for each picture of a paid trial, how many presses the trial still had to go,
learned as a class by a net of the reader's own shape from the reader's own paid trials. In
an unpaid trial the last press is taught unpaid; an earlier press is taught paid where the
picture after it is counted one press nearer payment than the picture before, and is
otherwise not taught. Nothing in it names a task.

Held through rung 4 and nothing at rung 5 (4/1431; at random 0/964), as with yesterday's
reader. The frames say why: through rung 4 the board is two rows of four tiles, at rung 5

Read after these (summaries only, nothing opened in full): Wu, Dyer and Neyshabur, "When do
curricula work?" (2021), where an ordering from easy to hard helps mainly when the budget is
short or the labels are noisy and little otherwise; and reports that relational or
object-centred policies carry over to larger boards where policies over fixed places do not
(Janisch et al.; Karia and Srivastava). Both agree with what was found; nothing new was taken.
For boards that change, the nearer thing is this project's own rule read round a place (63).

As written the condition asks, on three of five tasks, twice the share of the reader begun
anew. On short_tower that cannot be had: twice 0.67 is more than every trial. On turned_tiles
and marker_walk_shuffled neither way of starting is paid, for reasons that are not the length
of the walk (a board that changes; controls that are mixed). So three of five is out of reach
as written, and not for want of sub-goals. The weakest replacement that keeps what the
condition was for: a task counts when the climbing reader is paid at rung 5 at least twice
as often as presses at random, and at least twice as often as the reader begun anew there
with the same budget unless that reader is itself paid more than half the time, where twice
cannot be had and the task is simply learned. Three of five still asked. It is weaker in one
place only, and it says so: on such a task climbing is not shown to help.
