# Research diary, 12 November 2026

## Pong: a learner that reads every tick and decides every few

Design, from THEORY §52.11. The trainer in `nca-pong` now keeps one observation per tick in the history, decides every
three ticks and holds its action in between, and files the person's demonstrated keys per tick. The action marked at a
takeover is the one in force 0.25 seconds, five ticks, before the key press. The unjudged margin is two decisions, six
ticks. Admission plays fresh serves alone in the same way, with the state advanced every tick and the action changed at
decision ticks. The careful scripted teacher judges a decision against the ball when it was made; the sloppy one judges
every tick. The core learner is unchanged.

Tuning seeds 0 to 11, exploratory: careful 9 of 12, sloppy 11 of 12. The learner that read once per decision gave 7 and
6 on the same seeds.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 36 to 47, never run before. Same teaching procedure: blocks of 100
serves, stop at admission, limit 800, admission by the certified gate on 300 fresh serves. The bar is unchanged: at
least 8 of 12 admitted with the careful teacher and at least 6 of 12 with the sloppy one. Both must hold for the
learner to count as ready for people to teach it. Prediction: careful 8 to 10, sloppy 9 to 12.

Result, fresh seeds 36 to 47. Careful 7 of 12 (admitted 36, 38, 39, 41, 42, 43, 45; not 37, 40, 44, 46, 47). Sloppy
9 of 12 (not 37, 44, 47). The sloppy teacher clears its bar and the careful one misses by one seed, so the learner is
not ready. The prediction was right for the sloppy teacher and wrong for the careful one.

Diagnostic, tuning seed 0, careful teacher, 300 serves, exploratory. The careful teacher holds the key it chose at a
decision for the whole step, while the trainer now files a demonstration at every tick. Of the demonstrations filed
with the ball level, 72 at decision ticks are all "stay", but between decisions 24 are "down" and 22 are "up", chosen
when the ball was still below or above. With the ball below, 3 "up" between decisions, none at decision ticks. The
fault §52.11 removed from the agent's own steps reappears in the person's: a demonstration is a decision made at one
tick and held, so filing it at the ticks after misfiles it whenever the ball crossed a band in between.

## Judgements of uncertain time

THEORY §52.12 corrects §52.11: a judgement concerns a tick the learner does not know, and a shown action held from
before the step concerns none of the step's ticks. Four ways of filing, compared on tuning seeds 0 to 11, exploratory.
The decision interval, margin, teachers and procedure are as above.

| Filing | Careful | Sloppy |
|---|---|---|
| Every tick (§52.11) | 9 | 11 |
| Every tick where the ball stayed in one band since the decision, shown and played alike | 8 | 11 |
| Shown actions only at decision ticks, played at every tick | 11 | 10 |
| Shown actions only at decision ticks, played where the ball stayed in one band | 11 | 9 |

Filing shown actions where the ball did not move still lost the careful teacher three seeds. That teacher, taking
over mid-step, holds the key it chose at the previous decision, so the assumption that a judgement concerns a tick of
the current step fails for it. Filed only at decision ticks, where both scripted teachers have just chosen, it recovers.
Leaving the learner's own changed steps unjudged as well changes nothing measurable, so the design keeps the simpler
rule and the core is unchanged.

Preregistration, written before running. Shown actions filed only at decision ticks, played steps at every tick.
Fresh seeds 48 to 59, never run before; seeds 36 to 47 were spent on the design above and are not reused. Same
procedure and the same bar: at least 8 of 12 careful and at least 6 of 12 sloppy, both required. Prediction: careful 9
to 11, sloppy 8 to 11.

Result, fresh seeds 48 to 59. Careful 8 of 12 (not 48, 50, 52, 55). Sloppy 9 of 12 (not 50, 53, 55). Both bars hold,
so by the preregistered criterion the learner is ready for people to teach it. Careful lies at the bar's edge and below
the predicted 9 to 11; sloppy lies in its predicted range. Four of the seven failures end with three states, one per
band, so the learner found the right shape and some label in it is wrong; seeds 52 and 55 with the sloppy teacher grew
to 84 and 53 states. Over the two fresh sets, the careful teacher's misses are the weaker side.

## A real person reacts later than the scripted ones

The first person to teach the new trainer reported that the agent moved down always. Steps the person let pass before
the unjudged margin of two decisions, 0.3 seconds, count as good, and a person who watches the paddle go the wrong way
for a second before taking over approves the wrong action at every one of those steps. Exploratory, tuning seeds 0 to
3, 200 serves of a careful scripted teacher reacting in 0.8 to 1.6 seconds: none admitted, and the machines answer
"up" with the ball below or level. Reacting in 0.25 to 0.55 seconds, 3 of 4.

|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 of 6 | 5 of 6 |
| 10 | 5 of 6 | 5 of 6 |
| none of the learner's steps judged | 5 of 6 | 4 of 6 |

The trainer now uses ten decisions, 1.5 seconds. The preregistered pass on seeds 48 to 59 was at two decisions and does
not cover this setting, which has not been tested on fresh seeds.

## A faster paddle, and guessing instead of waiting

At the person's request the paddle moves twice as fast, 0.020 per tick, and where the learner has no action the agent
guesses one instead of waiting for a demonstration. Exploratory, tuning seeds, margin of ten decisions.

At the faster paddle one decision moves the paddle 0.06, the width of the level band, so the band was widened to
±0.06 as well. Moving toward the ball meets it on 300 of 300 serves at either width. Waiting, seeds 0 to 5: band ±0.03,
careful 4 of 6 and slow 5 of 6; band ±0.06, 5 and 5. The wider band is kept.

Seeds 0 to 11, band ±0.06:

| Where the learner has no action | Careful | Sloppy | Slow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wait for a demonstration | 10 | not run | 11 |
| Guess anew at every decision | 8 | 11 | 8 |
| Guess once per observation and hold it for the serve | 11 | 12 | 12 |

A guess redrawn every 0.15 seconds rarely stays wrong for as long as a person takes to object, and every chance match
restarts the person's patience, so few guesses are taken over and an episode no one took over teaches nothing. A guess
held for the serve stays wrong until the person objects. The held guess is kept. None of this has been tested on fresh
seeds.

## Tetris taught by takeover through judgements that carry (THEORY §53)

The Tetris package ranked placements by weights over board features chosen by hand: lines, heights, holes and
roughness. It now has none. For each placement the agent sees the piece and rotation as a context and the cells around
where the piece would land, its box with a column to each side and the row below, as filled or empty, 16 cells padded.
The core's `Relevance` keeps, per context, every set of at most four cells that no two judgements contradict, 2517 sets,
and the agent proposes the placement with the widest good majority, or guesses. A person who takes over judges the
proposal bad and their own placement good. The scripted teacher takes over when the proposal leaves an empty cell under
the piece and some placement would not, and shows the lowest such placement. Admission: 300 fresh games of ten pieces
played alone, each without a hole another placement would have avoided, within the certificate.

Exploratory, tuning seeds. Counting an unopposed proposal as judged good, as passed steps are in Pong, taught nothing:
on seed 0, 240 takeovers in 600 pieces and no admission, with 14 of 19 contexts left without a consistent set. On 98 of
those 600 pieces no placement avoided a hole, the teacher let the proposal pass, and the pass contradicted the
judgements against holes: the teacher's verdict there depends on the rest of the board, outside the cells seen, which
is the limit §53.4 names. With unopposed proposals left unjudged, takeovers on seed 0 were 51 in the first 250 pieces and
14 in the next 1750, and the agent was admitted.

Seeds 0 to 11, blocks of 250 pieces, limit 2000: the attentive teacher 12 of 12 admitted, after 500 to 2000 pieces and
49 to 65 takeovers; a teacher who notices a bad proposal seven times in ten, 10 of 12, after 52 to 66 takeovers. The
bound of 53.4 for these sizes is 2533 per context, far above what was used.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 12 to 23, the same procedure. Bar: at least 8 of 12 admitted with
the attentive teacher and at least 6 of 12 with the one attentive seven times in ten. Prediction: 11 to 12 and 8 to 11.

Result, fresh seeds 12 to 23. Attentive 11 of 12 (not 22), after 250 to 1750 pieces and 44 to 66 takeovers. Attentive
seven times in ten, 9 of 12 (not 12, 21, 22). Both bars hold and both lie in their predicted ranges. Across all 24
attentive runs, tuning and fresh, takeovers stayed between 44 and 66 however many pieces were played, so a person's
effort is set by the number of distinct situations that matter, not by the length of play.

## Tetris with a person: contradicted sets are weighed, not dropped (THEORY §53.5)

The first person to teach the board took over 313 times in 834 pieces, and the agent still guessed. Their saved learner
had no consistent set left in 16 of its 19 contexts. A person takes over when they prefer another placement, not only
when the proposal makes a hole, and judges by what the cells around a placement do not show, so their judgements
contradict every set and the learner of §53.3 is left guessing.

Reproduced on tuning seed 0 with a scripted teacher who also takes over when the proposal's top lies more than two rows
above the lowest placement without a hole: 654 takeovers in 1000 pieces, still rising, and no context with a set left.
The holes-only teacher took 61.

`Relevance` now gives each set the weight `keep` raised to its contradictions. Tuning seeds 0 to 11, blocks of 250
pieces, limit 2000:

| keep | Holes only: admitted | Holes only: takeovers | Also height: admitted | Also height: takeovers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 12 | 49–65 | 0 | 1216–1442 |
| 0.1 | 10 | 50–68 | 1 | 300–350 |
| 0.2 | 10 | 59–69 | 1 | 280–335 |
| 0.3 | 11 | 62–80 | 1 | 287–335 |
| 0.5 | 8 | 63–87 | 1 | 278–329 |
| 0.8 | 5 | 90–106 | 0 | 310–335 |

Weighing cuts the takeovers of the teacher who also judges height about fourfold, and costs the holes-only teacher a
little. That teacher's height objections are still not learned: whether a placement is too high depends on the best
placement elsewhere, and a good or bad mark on one placement cannot express a comparison. Adding the landing row to the
view did not help either (keep 0.5, seeds 0 and 1, height teacher: 196 and 255 takeovers in 1000 pieces against 183 and
193 without). `keep` 0.3 is taken.

Profiling found the votes, 71 million key reads in 300 pieces, and a context rebuilt on every lookup. Reading keys with
`itemgetter`, keeping weights and remembering votes until the context's next judgement made teaching 1000 pieces take 2
seconds instead of 29, with identical results.

Preregistration, written before running. `keep` 0.3, fresh seeds 24 to 35, same procedure. Holes-only teacher: at
least 8 of 12 admitted. Teacher who also judges height: median takeovers at most 400 over the run. Prediction: 10 to 12
admitted, and a median of 290 to 340.

Result, fresh seeds 24 to 35. Holes-only teacher: 12 of 12 admitted, after 59 to 76 takeovers. Teacher who also judges
height: median 310.5 takeovers over 2000 pieces (289 to 333), and none admitted. Both preregistered criteria hold and
both lie in the predicted ranges. The height teacher's objections remain unlearned, and the rate at which that teacher
takes over stays near one piece in seven; comparisons between placements are the next thing to model.

## Comparisons instead of marks (THEORY §53.6)

A takeover now may be recorded as the person's placement preferred to the proposal: a set wins in one class and loses in
the other, and is contradicted only where it ranked the two the wrong way. Prediction before measuring: the set that
judges holes stops being contradicted by height objections, so the height teacher's takeovers fall well below 300.
Exploratory, tuning seeds 0 to 11, `keep` 0.3, limit 2000; holes-only teacher admitted of 12, and the height teacher's
takeovers:

| Recording | Votes | Holes only | Height teacher |
|---|---|---|---|
| marks | sign | 11 | 287–335, 1 admitted |
| comparisons | sign | 10 | 267–336, 2 admitted |
| comparisons with the landing row seen | sign | 7 | 274–331, 1 admitted |
| marks | lean | 10 | 273–313, 0 admitted |
| comparisons | lean | 9 | 188–287, median about 268, 3 admitted |
| comparisons with the landing row seen | lean | 6 | 248–312, 0 admitted |

The prediction failed: comparisons alone changed little. Protecting the hole set from height objections does not stop
them, because the agent still cannot tell a high placement from a low one and keeps proposing high ones. With the row
in view, a vote of the sign of a class's record cannot rank two clean placements of nearby rows, so votes now carry
the share by which a class leans; that gives the best height-teacher result, about a tenth fewer takeovers. Even then
the row did not help: each row value is a symbol of its own, the learner has no notion that a lower row is better, and
which row is too high depends on the best placement on the board. Comparisons with lean-weighted votes are taken.

Preregistration, written before running. Comparisons, lean-weighted votes, `keep` 0.3, fresh seeds 36 to 47, the same
procedure. Holes-only teacher: at least 8 of 12 admitted. Height teacher: median takeovers at most 290, against the 310.5
of seeds 24 to 35 with marks. Prediction: 9 to 11 admitted, and a median of 255 to 285.

Result, fresh seeds 36 to 47. Holes-only teacher: 7 of 12 admitted (36 to 41: 38 and 40; 42 to 47: all but 44),
below the bar of 8. Height teacher: median 273 takeovers (239 to 290), within its bar and its predicted range. The
preregistered criterion fails on the holes-only teacher, so the change is not taken: Tetris keeps marks, votes of the
sign and `keep` 0.3, which met both bars on seeds 24 to 35, and the comparison code is removed. Comparisons bought about
a tenth fewer height objections at the cost of the hole judgements: a takeover recorded as a comparison carries one
lesson where marks carried two, and a set is no longer told that the proposal itself was bad. The height teacher's
objections stay unlearned under every recording tried. What would learn them is a notion of order over a component's
values, a lower row being better, which the learner does not have: each value is a symbol of its own.

## Ordered components: the direction of a preference is learned (THEORY §53.7)

The landing row of a placement joins the view as an ordered component. The learner is told only that its values can be
compared, and weighs "larger is preferred" against "smaller is preferred" from the takeovers. Exploratory, tuning seeds
0 to 11, `keep` 0.3, limit 2000; holes-only teacher, and the teacher who also objects to a proposal more than two rows
above its own choice:

| Version | Holes only | Height teacher |
|---|---|---|
| No row (the committed version) | 11 admitted, 62–80 takeovers | 1 admitted, 287–335 |
| Row; direction from the person's placement against the proposal | 8 admitted, 108–128 | 0 admitted, 210–258 |
| The same, choosing among placements not believed bad | 9 admitted, 104–121 | 1 admitted, 197–254 |
| Direction from the person's placement against every rival not believed bad | 0–1 admitted, 128–154 | 2–3 admitted, 127–170 |
| The same, with the row left out of the sets | 9 admitted, 95–109 | 9 admitted, 104–114 |

Comparing the person's placement with the proposal alone taught no direction: on seed 0 the two directions were
contradicted 43 times each with the holes-only teacher and 80 and 82 times with the height teacher. A placement that
makes a hole usually lands deeper than the clean one shown in its place, so every hole correction counted against
"deeper is preferred". Scored against every rival not believed bad, the costs on seed 0 were 19 and 103, and proposals
more than two rows too high fell to at most 2 in 250 pieces. With the row also among the sets as a symbol of twenty
values, hole judgements were learned much more slowly; an ordered component is now judged by its direction only.

Preregistration, written before running. The last row of the table, fresh seeds 48 to 59, the same procedure.
Holes-only teacher: at least 8 of 12 admitted. Height teacher: at least 8 of 12 admitted and a median of at most 200
takeovers, against 310.5 and none admitted on seeds 24 to 35. Prediction: 8 to 11 admitted for each, and a median of 100
to 120.

Result, fresh seeds 48 to 59. Holes-only teacher: 7 of 12 admitted, after 90 to 112 takeovers; the bar of 8 is missed.
Height teacher: 5 of 12 admitted, below the bar of 8, with a median of 113.5 takeovers (108 to 122), inside its bar of
200 and its predicted range. The admission criteria fail, and the prediction of 8 to 11 admitted was wrong for both
teachers; the takeover criterion holds. Against the committed version on its own fresh seeds, the height teacher goes
from none admitted and 310.5 takeovers to 5 admitted and 113.5, and the holes-only teacher from 12 admitted and 59 to
76 takeovers to 7 admitted and 90 to 112. The change is kept because a person teaches as the height teacher does, but
it has not met its bar, and the learner is not ready by the criterion set. What it loses is in the hole judgements:
taking the deepest placement believed good, rather than the one with the widest majority, proposes placements the
learner has weaker grounds for.

## Othello taught through relevance, with the learner's judgement of the reply

The Othello package is rebuilt as Pong and Tetris were. The agent plays Black against a sparring partner that plays by
a handwritten reading of the board, with one move in ten at random. For each legal move the agent sees how far the
square lies from the nearest edge each way and what stands on its eight neighbours, ten components, and learns through
`Relevance` with sets of at most three and `keep` 0.3. A scripted person takes over when the handwritten reading
values the proposal more than 1.5 below its best move, and plays that move. With no slack the person objected to about
six proposals in ten at every stage: the reading's finer preferences are not in the view. Exploratory, seed 0,
objections in games 100 to 150 of about 1,520 decisions:

| Version | Objections |
|---|---|
| Guessing only | 248 |
| Discs turned as an ordered component (§53.7), sets of three, four | 103, 83 |
| Discs turned as a symbol, sets of three, four | 59, 123 |
| No count of discs turned, sets of three, four | 52, 56 |
| The same, sets of three, a move valued less the best judgement of a reply | 22 |

The ordered count hurt: the person's corner moves turn few discs and their other moves many, and a learned direction
overrode the judgement that a corner is good. Nearly all objections left were to moves that give White a corner. The
last row values a move by its own judgement less the largest judgement the same learner gives any reply open to White,
read from White's side: seed 1 went from 52 to 16. On tuning seeds 0 to 3, teaching in blocks of 20 games until 300
decisions made alone in fresh games pass the certified gate (tolerance 0.05, confidence 0.001), the agent was admitted
after 20 to 40 games and 7 to 50 takeovers; without the reply, after 40 to 200 games and 67 to 229 takeovers. An
untaught agent fails the gate after 32 to 64 decisions. Over 3,000 further decisions alone, seeds 0 and 1: the person
would object to 28 and 31, against 443 and 453 untaught, and the agent won 79 of 98 and 69 of 99 games, against 13 of
100 and 9 of 99.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 20 to 31, the procedure above with a limit of 200 games. At
least 10 of 12 admitted, with a median of at most 60 takeovers. Over 3,000 decisions alone against a sparring partner
it has not met, the taught agents win a median of at least 60 in 100 games and the untaught at most 25. Without the
reply, the median of takeovers is at least twice that with it. Prediction: 12 admitted, a median of 15 to 45
takeovers, 65 to 80 games in 100 won.

Result, fresh seeds 20 to 31. All 12 admitted, after 20 to 100 games and a median of 37 takeovers (17 to 79). Over
3,000 decisions alone the person would object to 11 to 45 of the taught agents' and 402 to 476 of the untaught; the
taught won a median of 68.7 in 100 games (51 to 90) and the untaught 7 to 14. Without the reply, 11 of 12 were
admitted with a median of 99.5 takeovers (54 to 238), 2.7 times as many. Every criterion holds and every prediction
fell inside its range. Three taught agents won fewer than 60 in 100 (51, 56, 60): admission is by the person's
objections, and an agent the person lets stand is not yet one that beats the sparring partner every time. The reply's
judgement is computed in the package; the core's learner has no notion of an opponent.

## Snake taught through relevance

The Snake package is rebuilt the same way. The agent steers; a person who presses a direction before the snake moves
makes that move, which is judged good, and the proposal bad. The agent may propose a move into a wall or its own body:
the game withholds nothing. A move is seen along its own direction as nine components: the turn it makes, whether the
food lies ahead or behind and to which side, and six cells in front, each empty, food, body or wall. `Relevance`, sets
of at most three, `keep` 0.3. A scripted person steers when the proposal crashes, enters a region smaller than the
snake, or leaves the food where another move would not; the middle objection reads further than the six cells.
Exploratory, tuning seeds 0 and 1, teaching in blocks of 500 moves until 300 moves alone in fresh games pass the
certified gate (tolerance 0.05, confidence 0.001): admitted after the first block with 15 and 16 takeovers. Over 3,000
further moves alone the agents ate 282 and 248 times, against 9 and 13 untaught, and the person would object to 37 and
25 moves (23 and 13 crashes, 2 and 11 dead ends, 12 and 1 away from the food), against 1,574 and 1,566.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 20 to 31, the same procedure with a limit of 5,000 moves. At
least 10 of 12 admitted, with a median of at most 40 takeovers. Over 3,000 moves alone the taught agents eat a median
of at least 150 times and the untaught at most 30. Prediction: 12 admitted, a median of 10 to 25 takeovers, 220 to 300
eaten.

Result, fresh seeds 20 to 31. All 12 admitted after the first block, with a median of 26 takeovers (12 to 60). Over
3,000 moves alone the taught agents ate a median of 251 times (231 to 272) and the untaught 6 to 13; the person would
object to 21 to 46 of the taught agents' moves, of which 4 to 15 were dead ends, and to about 1,550 of the untaught.
Every criterion holds. The predicted median of 10 to 25 takeovers was missed by one; the other predictions held. The
taught agents still crash 6 to 19 times in 3,000 moves where another move was open: the snake that has coiled around
itself meets arrangements of the six cells no takeover has judged.

## Probes placed by what the board holds (THEORY §54.6), and a guard against circling

The probes of the previous entry were placed by hand: cells counted from the head along the move, and the side the
food lies on, ready-made. Here the agent is given the board as cells of four kinds (empty, food, body, head) and the
four directions as names. It is not told which kind is the head or the food, or what a direction does. Its pool is
every kind as a candidate anchor: the cell at each offset within two of where a kind stands alone, 96 probes, and
which way one kind lies from another along each axis, 12 probes. `Inquiry`, price 1, sets of at most three. The
search for a probe to take up was moved into array operations, since the plain count took seconds a judgement on this
pool.

All three learners now also refuse to repeat a move from a board they have been on since the snake last ate. Exploratory,
tuning seeds 0 to 11, the scripted person and the admission procedure as before:

## Snake from the screen's pixels (THEORY §54.6)

The agent is given the screen: 64 by 64 pixels in four colours, each cell a block of three by three with a gap, and
the four directions as names. An anchor is now a colour whose pixels form one solid block, placed at the block's least
corner; a cell standing alone was the smallest case. The pool is the pixel at each offset within five of each colour's
block, 480 probes, and the 12 bearings between colours. The agent is not told which colour is the head or the food,
how wide a cell is, or what a direction does. The search for a probe compares in full only the 24 that explain most
when joined to at most one probe of the view. Exploratory, tuning seeds 0 to 11, the procedure as before:

| Learner | Admitted | Takeovers, median (range) | Eaten in 3,000 moves alone | Probes in view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The screen's pixels | 11 | 105 (36–291) | 227–275 | 6–20 |
| The bare board, anchors as solid blocks | 12 | 198 (39–259) | 188–268 | 10–22 |
| The bare board, anchors as single cells (previous entry) | 11 | 139 (49–296) | 232–277 | 10–21 |

Every pixel view holds both bearings between food and head, and pixels exactly four from the head's corner, the
width of a cell, along an axis. On the bare board the new anchors did harm: two body cells side by side form a block,
and probes anchored on the body were taken up on 9 of 12 seeds. On the screen the gap between cells keeps the body
from ever forming one. The bare-board mode is removed from the Snake package; the screen replaces it.

Preregistration, written before running. Fresh seeds 56 to 67, the screen's pixels. At least 9 of 12 admitted within
5,000 moves; a median of at most 200 takeovers; a median of at least 200 eaten in 3,000 moves alone and no agent below
100; both bearings between food and head in at least 10 of 12 views; at least two of the four pixels one cell's width
from the head's corner in at least 10 of 12 views. Prediction: 10 to 12 admitted, a median of 70 to 160 takeovers, a
median of 240 to 270 eaten, a median view of 9 to 15 probes.

Result, fresh seeds 56 to 67. 10 of 12 admitted; a median of 178.5 takeovers (35 to 294); a median of 245.5 eaten in
3,000 moves alone (154 to 278), none below 100; both bearings between food and head in all 12 views; at least three of
the four pixels one cell's width from the head's corner in all 12; a median view of 15 probes (5 to 17) of 492. Every
criterion holds. The predicted median of 70 to 160 takeovers was wrong; the other predictions held. The two agents not
admitted ate 154 and 237 times, and the person would still object to 242 and 112 of their 3,000 moves, nearly all for
leaving the food. The views also hold pixels five from the head's corner, which read the same cell as those four away,
and one to six pixels near the food that the person's judgements do not depend on.
