# 80. A reading by which things recur

A reader that pairs two frames place by place reads what changed at a place. Some questions are
not of places but of things: which thing of this frame was in no earlier one, which is shown
once where the others are shown twice. This chapter gives a reading of that which is not taught,
says what it is unmoved by, what a pairing by places has to be taught in its stead, and on what
terms a part that reads it may be added to a reader that has been taught without it.

## 80.1 Model

A **frame** is a finite family of **things**, each a **picture** at a **place**. A **likeness**
gives two pictures a number from nought to one, and a picture is wholly like itself. Nothing is
said of how a frame is cut into things or of how the likeness is made, only that the likeness
turns on the two pictures and on nothing of where they lie.

How **new** a thing of the frame of now is against an earlier frame is one less the likeness of
the most alike thing of the earlier frame. How **alone** a thing is in its frame is how new it
is against the other things of that frame.

## 80.2 Unmoved by where things lie

**Proposition 80.2 (newness is unmoved).** Rearranging the things of the earlier frame over its
places leaves how new each thing of the frame of now is. A thing whose picture some thing of
the earlier frame has is not new at all.

Nor does a thing's own place enter. So where what is asked is the thing that was not there
before, and that thing is unlike every earlier one by some margin, how new it is exceeds how
new any other is by that margin in every arrangement of either frame, with no lesson taken.
The same holds of the thing shown once among things shown twice, by how alone it is.

## 80.3 What a pairing by places has to be taught

**Proposition 80.3 (the same place is moved).** Where no two things of the earlier frame are
the same, a rearrangement that moves a place changes what lies at it.

A reader that takes, at each place, the frame of now, the earlier frame and their difference
there is given everything, so it is not short of what decides. But the earlier frame of n
different things has n! arrangements with one answer and n! different readings place by place:
that these are the same is what such a reader must take from lessons, and what newness has by
its form. A task that arranges its things anew each time shows each arrangement about once.

## 80.4 A part added to a reader

Let a reader give each port an amount, and let a part reading how new and how alone things are
give each port an amount of its own, added to the reader's times a **factor**.

With the factor nought the reader is as it was. So the factor is fitted, with a penalty of half
its square, on lessons the part was not taught on, and by 79.2 the loss on those lessons is
then no more than without the part; and since a part that tells nothing seems to tell a little
on lessons its factor was fitted to, whether it tells at all is taken as in 79.2, from lessons
kept out of the fitting, and the factor is nought unless the gain a lesson exceeds its own
scatter.

The part is taught on its own amounts and not on what the reader leaves. The loss of a lesson
changes with the part's amount by the chance read less the payment, which is nought wherever
the reader already fits the lesson: a reader that fits its lessons at once leaves the part
nothing to be taught by, whatever the part could tell of lessons not yet met.
